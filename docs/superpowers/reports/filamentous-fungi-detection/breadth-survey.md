# Detecting Filamentous Fungi (Hyphae) as a Binary Mask on Macroscopic RGB Agar Plates — A Breadth-First Method Survey

**Problem framing.** Hyphae are thin, oriented, branching ridges on agar. Detection must key on local 2nd-derivative/phase **geometry**, not an intensity threshold. Two hard regimes dominate: (a) **low SNR** — faint filaments demand *integration of evidence along a line*; (b) **dense overlap** — crossings/junctions create gaps, and the canonical Hessian filters (Frangi/Sato) actively *suppress* junctions. Confounders are **chromatic aberration** (color fringes that mimic thin colored filaments) and **agar grain**. Target operating point: training-free → small-data, a single tunable knob, and gap-bridging. The reference **baseline** is phase congruency + background subtraction + hysteresis + Dijkstra least-cost linking.

## 2. Per-Family Method Profiles

### 2.1 Hessian Vesselness & Junction Suppression

**Frangi vesselness.** *Math:* At each scale σ, compute the Hessian H (Gaussian-smoothed 2nd derivatives); eigenvalues |λ1|≤|λ2|. In 2D, V=0 if λ2>0 (for a bright ridge on dark bg), else V = exp(−Rᵦ²/2β²)·(1−exp(−S²/2c²)), with blobness Rᵦ=|λ1|/|λ2| and structureness S=√(λ1²+λ2²); multiscale = max over σ. *Used for:* retinal/MRA/CT angiography enhancement — the canonical tubularity filter. *How it works:* smooth at σ → build Hessian → eigendecompose for ridge geometry → map eigenvalue ratios to a score → max across σ to span radii. *Hyphae relevance:* strong training-free thin-ridge detector with one effective sensitivity knob (c/β), but the eigenvalue model **collapses at junctions** — both eigenvalues grow large at X/T crossings, driving V→0 and breaking the network exactly where dense hyphae overlap [arxiv 1709.05495]. Use as a baseline ridge channel, not for junction integrity. *Source:* [link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/bfb0056195], [arxiv.org/pdf/1709.05495].

**Sato tubularity.** *Math:* Hessian-eigenvalue-based but uses a directional/asymmetric line measure rather than Frangi's ratio. With sorted eigenvalues (λ1≈along-vessel≈0; cross-eigenvalues strongly negative), Sato weights |λ_cross| and modulates by the sign/magnitude of the smaller cross-eigenvalue with separate factors (α1, α2) for the λc<0 vs λc>0 regimes — deliberately asymmetric to tolerate real-vessel contrast variation; scales integrated by equalizing per-scale noise. *Used for:* segmentation/visualization of curvilinear structures (MRA vessels, CT bronchi). *How it works:* Gaussian-smooth → Hessian → eigendecompose, identify cross-section plane → score "lineness" with asymmetric α-weighting → integrate scales with noise equalization. *Hyphae relevance:* a 2nd-derivative ridge filter (no training, σ spans hypha width) whose asymmetric weighting handles **low contrast** better than Frangi — but it shares the eigenvalue-collapse junction weakness, so it bridges low-SNR lines yet not overlap gaps. *Source:* [link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/bfb0029240], [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10646760/].

**Jerman enhancement.** *Math:* a **ratio** of multiscale Hessian eigenvalues rather than Frangi's products. Define regularized λρ (clamped λ2 in 2D relative to a fraction τ of the image-max |λ2|), then V = λ2²·(λρ−λ2)·[3/(λ2+λρ)]³, normalized to [0,1] and maxed over σ; the τ-regularization lifts response at edges/junctions. *Used for:* vascular enhancement where Frangi is non-uniform across radii or weak at bifurcations/aneurysms. *How it works:* multiscale Hessian + eigenvalues → regularize 2nd eigenvalue against τ·max → ratio-based vesselness → max over scales. *Hyphae relevance:* the **best junction-preserving Hessian option here** — the eigenvalue-ratio model **reinforces response at bifurcations** and enhances vessel-vs-background borders, directly targeting the dense-overlap regime while staying training-free with a single τ knob [hal-02544493v2; SPIE 2015 9413E.2A]. *Source:* [ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015SPIE.9413E..2AJ], [hal.science/hal-02544493v2].

**Meijering neuriteness.** *Math:* a **modified Hessian** — eigenvalues recombined as λ′ = λ + α·λ_orthogonal (canonically α=−1/3); neuriteness is normalized λ′/λ′min over the image. *Used for:* tracing thin filaments in low-contrast fluorescence microscopy. *How it works:* Hessian at σ → form modified eigenvalue via α-combination → normalize by most-negative modified eigenvalue → max over σ. *Hyphae relevance:* explicitly devised for **low-SNR, intensity-inhomogeneous** filaments — exactly the faint-hyphae regime — with a single shaping knob α (default −1/3); junction handling is no better than Frangi-class. *Source:* [10.1002/cyto.a.20022], [hal.science/hal-02544493v2].

**Bowler-hat transform.** *Math:* pure morphology (no Hessian). Two SE banks — disks (diameter d) and oriented lines (length d, many θ). Per scale, I_disk=opening(I, disk_d), I_line=max_θ opening(I, line_{d,θ}); B = max over scales of (I_line − I_disk), enhancing pixels where a line SE survives but a disk SE does not. *Used for:* 2D/3D vessel enhancement emphasizing radius uniformity and junction robustness. *How it works:* open with growing disks (kill thin structure) → open with matching oriented lines (preserve aligned thin structure) → per-scale line−disk difference → combine across scales/orientations. *Hyphae relevance:* uniquely **junction-preserving** — longer line SEs still fit across a junction, so crossings are enhanced as brightly as the limbs joining them; training-free, single dominant knob (max SE length), bridges crossings the Hessian filters suppress [arxiv 1709.05495]. *Source:* [arxiv.org/pdf/1709.05495], [arxiv.org/abs/1802.05097].

### 2.2 Steger, Steerable & Matched Filters

**Steger unbiased curvilinear detector.** *Math:* build the 2×2 Hessian from Gaussian-smoothed partials; eigenvalues λ1,λ2 (|λ1|≥|λ2|) and the eigenvector **n** of λ1 give the line normal; the sub-pixel line point is where the 2nd directional derivative along **n** is maximal (vanishing-first-derivative Taylor test). *Used for:* sub-pixel curvilinear extraction — roads/rivers, DNA/vessel/fiber tracing, road-markings. *How it works:* convolve with Gaussian-derivative kernels (σ≳line-width/√3) → form H, take max-|λ| eigenvector as normal, locate sub-pixel ridge → link by **hysteresis double threshold** on the 2nd directional derivative → at a point already claimed by another line, mark a **junction** and split → connected lines-and-junctions network. *Hyphae relevance:* strong fit — geometry not intensity, native upper/lower hysteresis knob, **explicit junction preservation** (the Frangi/Sato weakness) and sub-pixel localization for thin hyphae. *Source:* [howardzzh.com/research/papers/vision/1998.PAMI.Steger.UnbiasedDetector.pdf].

**Steerable filters.** *Math:* a filter at angle θ is a fixed linear combination of a small basis: 1st-deriv-of-Gaussian G_θ=cosθ·Gx+sinθ·Gy; higher orders (G2 ridge + Hilbert pair H2) need more bases. The θ-response is closed-form, giving dominant orientation and oriented energy analytically per pixel without re-filtering. *Used for:* orientation analysis, oriented edge/ridge detection, adaptive anisotropic filtering, texture/flow. *How it works:* convolve once per basis filter → interpolate response at any θ in closed form → solve analytically for the orientation maximizing response → take that oriented/quadrature energy as ridge strength. *Hyphae relevance:* cheap dense per-pixel orientation + a quadrature-energy ("oriented strength") map that is a natural single threshold for a binary mask; the orientation field also seeds gap-bridging linking. *Source:* [10.1109/34.93808]; Canny-criterion 2D ridge/edge design [10.1109/TPAMI.2004.44].

**Matched filters.** *Math:* model the line cross-section as a Gaussian gray-level profile along a piecewise-linear segment; correlate with a zero-mean (DC-removed) rotated template. A bank of K rotated templates (classically 12, every 15°) spans directions; per-pixel response = max over orientations. *Used for:* retinal vessel segmentation (the canonical MF detector) and template-correlation detection of known cross-sections. *How it works:* design one Gaussian-profile zero-mean template → rotate into a 12-template bank → correlate each → take max over orientations and threshold (often MF-FDOG/hysteresis). *Hyphae relevance:* integrates evidence *along* the line via the template (boosts low-SNR ridges), training-free, one tunable correlation threshold — but the fixed Gaussian profile and discrete bank blur/suppress junctions and adapt poorly to variable width. *Source:* [10.1109/42.34715]; unsupervised complementary fusion (MF+Frangi+Gabor) [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4769136/].

**B-COSFIRE.** *Math:* configure DoG subunits at positions (ρ_i, φ_i) along a prototype line; response = **weighted geometric mean** of blurred, shifted DoG responses pooled collinearly, r=(Π_i s_i^{w_i})^{1/Σw_i}. Rotation-invariance via rotating φ_i; the AND-like geometric mean enforces collinear evidence. *Used for:* vessel/curvilinear delineation, elongated-pattern and bifurcation detection; trainable by example. *How it works:* present one prototype to auto-configure subunits → blur+shift each DoG toward the support center → combine by weighted geometric mean (strong only where collinear evidence co-occurs) → max over rotations, threshold (a symmetric "bar" variant detects bifurcations). *Hyphae relevance:* geometric-mean collinear pooling integrates oriented evidence for low-SNR hyphae, a dedicated symmetric filter targets junctions/bifurcations, and it configures from a single prototype (small-data) with one response-threshold knob. *Source:* [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25240643/].

**Gabor filter banks.** *Math:* Gabor kernel = oriented sinusoid × Gaussian envelope, g=exp(−(x'²+γ²y'²)/2σ²)·cos(2πx'/λ+ψ); a bank spans θ_k × (λ,σ); per-pixel feature = magnitude/energy pooled over the bank. Log-Gabor variants remove DC and better cover high frequencies. *Used for:* texture analysis, oriented edge/vessel enhancement, fingerprint ridges, fine curvilinear features. *How it works:* build the bank over orientations × scales → convolve → take magnitude and reduce (max/energy) per pixel → threshold the energy map; orientation = arg-max kernel. *Hyphae relevance:* tunable oriented bandpass enhances thin/low-contrast hyphae across scales (complements noise-robust Frangi in fusion), training-free, energy-threshold single knob — but junction response is weak and a dense bank is compute-heavy. *Source:* [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4769136/].

### 2.3 Phase Congruency & Optimally Oriented Flux

**Phase congruency (Kovesi).** *Math:* a bank of log-Gabor quadrature filters over n scales × m orientations gives per-filter amplitude A_n and phase φ_n; local energy E=√((ΣA_n cosφ_n)²+(ΣA_n sinφ_n)²); PC = E/(ΣA_n+ε), refined as PC2 = Σ_o Σ_n W·⌊A_n(cos(φ_n−φ̄)−|sin(φ_n−φ̄)|)−T⌋ / (ΣA_n+ε), with W a frequency-spread weight and T an estimated noise floor. *Used for:* illumination/contrast-invariant edge and ridge detection, corner/feature marking. *How it works:* convolve with the log-Gabor bank → per orientation compute energy and mean phase, then weighted noise-thresholded congruency → sum over orientations, normalize by total amplitude (0–1 map) → threshold/NMS. *Hyphae relevance:* the dimensionless 0–1 measure gives a single near-universal threshold (the "single knob") and is contrast-invariant, so faint hyphae over agar register without per-image intensity tuning — but it is per-pixel and does not itself bridge gaps or resolve junctions. **This is the baseline's front end.** *Source:* [peterkovesi.com/papers/phasecongtutorial.pdf].

**Monogenic / Riesz phase congruency.** *Math:* replace the oriented log-Gabor bank with an isotropic bandpass × the 2D Riesz transform (frequency response (u,v)/|(u,v)|); the monogenic signal (f, R_x f, R_y f) yields amplitude A=√(f²+R_x²+R_y²), phase φ=atan2(√(R_x²+R_y²), f), orientation θ=atan2(R_y f, R_x f) analytically per scale; PC=E/(ΣA+ε) across scales. *Used for:* rotation-covariant local phase/amplitude/orientation; edge/ridge detection without an orientation sweep. *How it works:* bandpass at several scales → Riesz transform for the two odd channels → per-scale amplitude/phase/orientation → accumulate congruency, normalize. *Hyphae relevance:* lower compute/memory than log-Gabor PC with better localization and anti-noise behavior, and it returns orientation for free — useful for low-SNR thin ridges and to feed orientation into a gap-bridging step, though still single-channel. *Source:* [researchgate.net/publication/269295895].

**Color / multichannel phase congruency (CMPCM).** *Math:* lift the color image into a conformal (stereographic) monogenic framework so phase congruency is computed jointly across channels in a geometric-algebra representation; edges are maxima of conformal-monogenic PC. *Used for:* color/multichannel edge detection with curvature-aware local-structure analysis. *How it works:* map color signal to conformal monogenic → per-scale conformal-monogenic phase/amplitude → locate PC maxima across channels → threshold. *Hyphae relevance:* directly targets the **chromatic-aberration / color-channel confound** the single-channel baseline ignores — fusing RGB before congruency can separate true hyphal ridges from color-fringe artifacts; reported to beat Canny/LOG/PC/MPC on Pratt's Figure of Merit. *Source:* [link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11042-018-6617-x].

**Optimally Oriented Flux (OOF).** *Math:* for a sphere of radius r at x, measure the gradient flux through the surface, f(x,r)=∮ (g(x+ρn̂)·n̂)(n̂·n̂)dA, reducing to a symmetric oriented-flux matrix Q_r(x) of image 2nd derivatives convolved with a sphere-*surface* (not solid-Gaussian) kernel; eigenvalues λ1≤λ2 describe flux along principal axes (a tube of the right radius gives strongly negative cross-section eigenvalues). *Used for:* scale/radius-adaptive curvilinear detection and radius estimate r*. *How it works:* per candidate r build Q_r(x) → eigendecompose → combine eigenvalues into a vesselness-like response, pick the radius maximizing it → threshold. *Hyphae relevance:* measures flux at the boundary of a local sphere rather than integrating over the structure, so it **resolves closely adjacent hyphae** and recovers weak low-SNR ridges that Frangi/Sato merge or miss — strong for dense-overlap + low-SNR, with σ fixed so edge sharpness is preserved. *Source:* [cse.hkust.edu.hk/~achung/eccv08_law_chung.pdf].

**OOF antisymmetry.** *Math:* augment the symmetric oriented-flux matrix with an **antisymmetric (odd) oriented-flux** measure: the symmetric part captures even, ridge-centered 2nd-order flux; the antisymmetric component captures odd, step-like flux that is large at a structure boundary/edge and ~zero at the centerline. Combining (e.g. OOFA weighting) penalizes responses sitting on edges. *Used for:* suppressing spurious edge/boundary responses, improving centerline localization in OOF tube detection. *How it works:* compute symmetric OOF and antisymmetric response per radius → flag boundary/asymmetric locations by antisymmetric magnitude → down-weight symmetric vesselness there → keep maxima centered on tubes. *Hyphae relevance:* improves centerline precision and rejects **agar-edge / plate-rim and chromatic-fringe step responses** (which are antisymmetric) while keeping true symmetric hyphal ridges — directly attacks the agar-grain/aberration confounders and yields a cleaner skeleton seed. *Source:* [cse.hkust.edu.hk/~achung/eccv08_law_chung.pdf].

### 2.4 Oriented Voting, RHT & Radon (Low-SNR Line Integration)

**Rolling Hough Transform (RHT).** *Math:* per-pixel orientation distribution R(θ,x,y). Unsharp-mask (top-hat smoothing of diameter D_K, subtract, threshold to a binary bitmask), then run a Hough restricted to ρ=0 in a circular window of diameter D_W; record θ iff Z·D_W of the on-bits lie along θ. Knob: sensitivity Z∈(0,1]. *Used for:* mapping faint diffuse linear structure (interstellar HI filaments, magnetic-field orientation) below the noise floor. *How it works:* unsharp-mask → bitmask → roll a circular window → accumulate a 1-D Hough of θ at ρ=0 → threshold by Z to emit per-pixel orientation(s). *Hyphae relevance:* integrates evidence *along* a line so it keys on coherent linearity not brightness (low-SNR fit) with exactly one recall/precision knob Z; weakness — collapses orientation to a single expectation per pixel, losing junction/branch info. *Source:* [arxiv.org/pdf/1312.1338], [10.1088/0004-637X/789/1/82].

**Radon / Hough line transform.** *Math:* Radon R(ρ,θ)=∫∫ f·δ(ρ−x cosθ−y sinθ) dx dy — line integrals of intensity; the discrete-binary dual is the Hough accumulator voting each edge pixel into its sinusoid ρ=x cosθ+y sinθ. Lines = peaks; RHT is the special case ρ=0 in a rolling window. *Used for:* global straight-line/edge detection, deskew, tomographic reconstruction, FilDReaMS oriented-bar filament integration. *How it works:* edge/feature map → each feature votes a sinusoid (or integrate intensity along every line) → find accumulator maxima → back-project peaks to segments. *Hyphae relevance:* the canonical line-*integration* operator — summing along a hypothesized path lifts a faint coherent ridge above per-pixel noise; a windowed/oriented-bar variant (FilDReaMS) recovers multiple per-pixel maxima → resolves crossings/branches and yields a mask, training-free via Monte-Carlo significance; pure global Radon is too coarse for curved/branching hyphae. *Source:* [aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2022/12/aa43506-22/aa43506-22.html].

**Tensor voting.** *Math:* encode each token as a 2nd-order symmetric tensor decomposed as T=(λ₁−λ₂)·ê₁ê₁ᵀ (stick/curve saliency)+λ₂·(ê₁ê₁ᵀ+ê₂ê₂ᵀ) (ball/junction saliency); tokens cast orientation-weighted votes through a decaying field (Gaussian in distance, penalizing curvature), tensor-summed and re-decomposed. λ₁−λ₂≫0 ⇒ on a curve; λ₁≈λ₂≫0 ⇒ junction/region. *Used for:* perceptual grouping / curve & surface inference from sparse noisy points; boundary inference, inpainting, gap-filling. *How it works:* seed tensors from local evidence → each token propagates a stick/ball vote → accumulate by tensor addition → eigen-decompose to read curve saliency, orientation, junctions. *Hyphae relevance:* the voting field **bridges gaps** across faint/broken ridges (low-SNR + gap-bridging) and, uniquely, the ball-tensor component explicitly flags junctions/branches that Frangi/Sato suppress; non-iterative, training-free, neighborhood scale σ as the single knob. *Source:* [sci.utah.edu/~gerig/CS7960-S2010/handouts/Medioni_tensor_voting.pdf].

**Line Segment Detector (LSD).** *Math:* build the level-line field (gradient-tangent angle per pixel); region-grow connected pixels whose orientations agree within tolerance τ into "line-support regions," fit a rectangle, validate by Number of False Alarms NFA=(N·M)^{5/2}·γ·B(n,k,p), accept iff NFA≤ε (B = binomial tail over aligned pixels). *Used for:* parameter-free local straight-segment detection (no Hough, no Canny). *How it works:* level-line orientation field → region-grow same-angle pixels → fit rectangle, count aligned points → keep segments with NFA≤ε. *Hyphae relevance:* training-free with a single principled sensitivity knob ε (a-contrario FDR), keys on oriented geometry not intensity; limitation — detects *straight* segments, so curved hyphae must be approximated piecewise and junctions split segments rather than being labeled. *Source:* [10.1109/tpami.2008.300].

**A-contrario validation.** *Math:* Helmholtz principle — under a null/noise model H0 (e.g. uniform-random gradient orientations), bound the expected occurrences of a geometric event; a structure with k aligned of n pixels is ε-meaningful iff NFA=N_tests·P_{H0}(≥k of n aligned)≤ε; expected ε-meaningful detections on pure noise ≤ε. *Used for:* detection-threshold-free validation of alignments, segments, contours, vanishing points — the statistical core under LSD. *How it works:* define H0 → enumerate candidates and their test multiplicity N_tests → compute the tail probability under H0 → accept only NFA≤ε. *Hyphae relevance:* a proven low-SNR / FDR-style threshold — bounds false hyphal detections on agar grain and chromatic-aberration noise directly, giving exactly one interpretable knob ε (expected false alarms), training-free. *Source:* [10.1109/tpami.2008.300].

### 2.5 Morphology, Path-Openings, Curvelets & Active Contours

**Path openings/closings.** *Math:* over an oriented adjacency graph, define a family of nearly-straight but flexible *paths* π; a path opening of length L is PO_L(f)(x)=max over admissible paths π∋x with |π|≥L of (min_{y∈π} f(y)); the dual path closing filters dark curvilinear structure on bright bg. Unlike a straight-line opening, the "element" is a set of flexible paths, so curved/tortuous ridges survive. *Used for:* crack/fiber/road/vessel filtering — keep thin long tortuous structures while erasing blobs and short noise. *How it works:* build the oriented DAG → per orientation compute the longest admissible path length through each pixel (linear-time parsimonious algorithm) → keep pixels with max path length ≥ L (the single knob) → max over orientations. *Hyphae relevance:* training-free, single length knob L; preserves long tortuous hyphal ridges and kills agar-grain blobs; the *incomplete/robust* variant tolerates gaps along a path, directly bridging signal-dropout — a strong low-SNR + gap fit. *Source:* [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24569442/].

**RORPO (Ranking the Orientation Responses of Path Operators).** *Math:* compute path openings along ~4 (2D) / ~7 (3D) discrete orientations; at each pixel sort the responses PO^{θ1}≥…≥PO^{θN}; the curvilinear indicator is RORPO=PO^{θ1}−PO^{θk} (a true 1-D line is bright in few orientations → large rank gap; a blob/plate is bright in many → gap ~0). Non-linear, non-local, parameter-light. *Used for:* blood-vessel and other curvilinear filtering — a morphological alternative to Hessian filters. *How it works:* path-open at each orientation → rank responses per pixel → difference high vs low ranks to score line-likeness → threshold/hysteresis. *Hyphae relevance:* avoids Hessian's local-neighborhood weakness at junctions/anisotropy; reported up to +8% TP / −50% FP vs Frangi/OOF — strong junction-gap + training-free fit, path-length as the knob. *Source:* [10.1109/tpami.2017.2672972].

**Grayscale top-hat.** *Math:* white top-hat WTH(f)=f−(f∘B); black/bottom-hat BTH(f)=(f•B)−f; the opening removes bright features that cannot contain B, so the residual isolates structures thinner than B. For oriented thin structures use a union of rotated linear SEs (max over angles). *Used for:* uneven-illumination correction; small-bright-feature extraction (microscopy spots, vessels, text, defects). *How it works:* open with B sized just larger than hyphal width → subtract from original → optionally repeat over rotated linear B's, max per pixel → threshold/hysteresis. *Hyphae relevance:* cheap training-free background subtraction + thin-bright enhancer (handles agar illumination gradient), single knob (B size); but the local opening does not integrate evidence along a line, so it is weaker than path operators at low SNR and at bridging gaps. *Source:* [sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0262885606002575].

**Curvelet transform.** *Math:* a multiscale, multi-orientation tight frame c(j,ℓ,k)=⟨f, φ_{j,ℓ,k}⟩ with anisotropic needle-shaped atoms obeying **parabolic scaling** (width≈length²), near-optimally sparse for C²-curved edges; FDCT (USFFT/wrapping) implementation; orientation index ℓ encodes edge direction. *Used for:* edge-preserving denoising, enhancement, and detection where structures are smoothly curved (vessels, horizons, fibers). *How it works:* FDCT → keep fine-scale coefficients with locally coherent orientation (or shrink/threshold the rest) → reconstruct enhanced/denoised image, or read dominant orientation+magnitude as a ridge map → threshold. *Hyphae relevance:* anisotropic atoms align with thin curved hyphae and concentrate their energy, so curvelet-domain thresholding denoises low-SNR images while *preserving* oriented ridges; per-angle energy resists agar grain — but no native gap/junction model, and the knob is a shrinkage threshold. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1137/05064182X].

**Shearlet transform.** *Math:* like curvelets but orientation from a **shear** S_s=[[1,s],[0,1]] composed with parabolic anisotropic scaling A_a, giving atoms ψ_{a,s,t}(x)=a^{-3/4}ψ(A_a^{-1}S_s^{-1}(x−t)); an affine (often compactly supported) frame with optimally sparse cartoon-image approximation and edge/ridge characterization via coefficient decay across scales (the maximizing shear gives orientation). *Used for:* edge/ridge detection, denoising, geometric separation; vessel/curvilinear enhancement. *How it works:* apply the discrete shearlet transform → at fine scales locate persistent large coefficients (edge/ridge points), read the maximizing shear as orientation → build an oriented-response enhancement or denoise by thresholding → threshold/hysteresis. *Hyphae relevance:* shear-based orientation is **lattice-exact** (better than rotated filters on grainy plates), sparsifies thin curved ridges for low-SNR denoise, and orientation persistence across scale gives robust ridge points; like curvelets, a shrinkage knob and no built-in junction/gap topology. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1016/j.acha.2007.09.003].

**Active contours / snakes (incl. open stretching, SOAC).** *Math:* a parametric curve v(s) minimizes E=∫[α|v'|²+β|v''|²]ds+∫E_ext(v)ds, with E_ext=−|∇(G_σ*I)|² (or a ridge/GVF force); Euler–Lagrange gives a gradient-descent PDE. Open/stretching active contours add an elongation force so the curve grows *along* a ridge centerline. *Used for:* boundary delineation and (open snakes) tracing 1-D filament/centerline networks (cytoskeleton, neurites). *How it works:* seed curves on intensity ridges (threshold τ) → evolve under smoothness + ridge force + stretch k_str → detect collisions and cut/splice at T-junctions → output centerlines → dilate to a mask. *Hyphae relevance:* open stretching snakes natively trace branching centerlines and **explicitly resolve junctions/crossings**, and stretch bridges short gaps — strong junction-gap fit; but two knobs (ridge τ + stretch k_str) need per-SNR retuning, so not single-knob, and they need decent initialization. *Source:* [nature.com/articles/srep09081].

**Level sets.** *Math:* represent the contour implicitly as the zero level set of φ(x,t); evolve ∂φ/∂t=|∇φ|(ακ+V_ext), κ=div(∇φ/|∇φ|) (curvature smoothing), V_ext from edges (geodesic g=1/(1+|∇G_σ*I|²)) or regions (Chan–Vese (I−c1)²−(I−c2)²); topology changes handled automatically. *Used for:* region/edge segmentation with automatic topology, vessel/multi-object splitting. *How it works:* initialize φ from a seed/threshold → compute speed from edges or regions → iterate the PDE with reinitialization → take φ≤0 (or a thin-tube variant) as the mask. *Hyphae relevance:* automatic topology merges fragmented segments and follows branches without re-seeding (helps gaps/junctions); region-based Chan–Vese needs no strong edges (good at low SNR) — but it is region/blob-oriented, multi-weight, slow, and not intrinsically a thin-ridge/single-knob detector. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1109/83.935033].

### 2.6 Diffusion Preprocessing & Chromatic-Aberration Rejection

**Coherence-enhancing diffusion (Weickert).** *Math:* anisotropic PDE ∂ₜu=div(D∇u) with D built from the structure tensor Jρ=Gρ*(∇uσ∇uσᵀ); sharing Jρ's eigenvectors (v₁∥gradient, v₂∥coherence), D sets λ₁=α (small, across) and λ₂=α+(1−α)·exp(−C/κ²) (large, along), where coherence κ=(μ₁−μ₂)² drives steering. *Used for:* restoring/closing fingerprint ridges, fabric weaves, fluid-flow line textures. *How it works:* compute Jρ and eigendecompose → set D to diffuse strongly along v₂, weakly along v₁ → evolve u a few iterations → read the smoothed, gap-closed image. *Hyphae relevance:* directly **bridges intensity gaps** by smoothing *along* hyphal orientation (its stated goal of "closing interrupted lines in flow-like images"); single coherence knob κ; training-free preprocessor feeding the binary-mask stage. *Source:* [link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1008009714131], [sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0262885698001024].

**Vesselness-enhancing diffusion (incl. Phase-Congruency-Tensor variant).** *Math:* a vesselness measure (Frangi from λ₁,λ₂) gates an anisotropic-diffusion tensor; the PCT variant replaces the intensity Hessian with a Phase Congruency Tensor (log-Gabor phase energy), whose phase sets diffusion direction and whose eigenvalues set a contrast-independent vesselness Vpc controlling diffusivity. *Used for:* enhancing tubular networks — retinal vessels, angiograms, and (validated) saprotrophic fungal/mycelial networks. *How it works:* multi-orientation phase congruency → build PCT → eigen-analyze for orientation+vesselness → diffuse along the vessel direction scaled by vesselness → iterate to lengthen ridges before thresholding. *Hyphae relevance:* phase-based ⇒ insensitive to along-hypha intensity variation and robust in low SNR (higher CNR than intensity coherence as noise rises); **validated on fungal networks** — but inherits the Hessian junction/branch-point dropout, so does not fix crossing gaps. *Source:* [markfricker.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/obara_et_al-2012-isbi-06235519.pdf], [10.1109/ISBI.2012.6235519].

**Structure-tensor analysis.** *Math:* local second-moment matrix J=Gρ*(∇I∇Iᵀ); eigenvalues μ₁≥μ₂ give orientation θ=½atan2(2Jxy, Jxx−Jyy) and coherence c=((μ₁−μ₂)/(μ₁+μ₂))²∈[0,1]; eigenvector v₂ is the dominant line direction. *Used for:* orientation/anisotropy estimation, corner & junction detection, optic-flow, texture — the engine under both diffusion filters. *How it works:* smoothed gradient outer-products → Gaussian-average over ρ → eigendecompose 2×2 J per pixel → emit orientation field + coherence map. *Hyphae relevance:* supplies per-pixel hyphal orientation and a single coherence knob (ρ scale) that downstream line-integration / least-cost gap-bridging follows; training-free; at junctions both eigenvalues rise (μ₁≈μ₂, c→0), **flagging branch points** rather than failing silently. *Source:* [link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1008009714131].

**Chromatic-aberration / lateral-color correction.** *Math:* model lateral color as a per-channel geometric warp relative to green — radial shift Δr=a·r+b·r³+c·r⁵ (radial-polynomial fit from optical center), or a dense warp maximizing R↔G and B↔G registration; resample R,B onto G's grid via sub-pixel interpolation. *Used for:* removing magnification-difference color fringing in cameras, microscopy, fringe-projection metrology. *How it works:* detect high-contrast edges/keypoints → fit a radial-polynomial (or dense) R→G, B→G displacement model → warp/resample R,B to register with G → residual desaturation. *Hyphae relevance:* removes the lateral-color confounder that mimics thin colored filaments **before** ridge detection, so geometry-based detectors don't lock onto aberration edges; one global model, training-free, parameterized by fitted radial coefficients. *Source:* [link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-53842-1_2], [researchgate.net/publication/2416871].

**Cross-channel consistency / fringe suppression.** *Math:* exploit a cross-channel prior that true edges are co-located/co-oriented across R,G,B; detect fringe where the color gradient is large but inconsistent with luminance/green gradient (∇C·∇G small), e.g. a purple-fringe mask from chroma + gradient direction; correct by desaturation toward G or a shearlet-domain / 5D direction-aware LUT alignment of R,B to G. *Used for:* purple/longitudinal-CA fringe removal along high-contrast edges; cross-channel deblurring/demosaicing. *How it works:* per-channel gradients and chroma → flag pixels where color gradient is strong but cross-channel-inconsistent with G → desaturate / re-align R,B to G there → leave consistent edges untouched. *Hyphae relevance:* targets **longitudinal/purple-fringe (LCA)** that residual lateral correction misses — suppresses colored pseudo-filaments along bright agar/colony edges while preserving genuine achromatic hyphal ridges; single chroma/consistency threshold, training-free (LUT variants need small data). *Source:* [arxiv.org/html/2511.12066v1], [link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-69532-3_7], [researchgate.net/publication/229015859].

### 2.7 U-Net Thin-Structure Segmentation & Class Imbalance

**U-Net.** *Math:* a symmetric encoder–decoder CNN minimizing a per-pixel loss (CE or soft-Dice); encoder `conv→ReLU→2×2 max-pool` (channels double), decoder `up-conv→concat(skip)→conv`; the key operator is the **skip connection** fusing same-resolution encoder/decoder maps; final `1×1 conv + sigmoid` gives per-pixel foreground probability. *Used for:* biomedical binary/semantic segmentation from small annotated datasets. *How it works:* contract to coarse semantic features → expand, concatenating skip features to recover boundaries → predict per-pixel probability, threshold. *Hyphae relevance:* the natural backbone for a learned binary hyphae mask; skip connections preserve thin-ridge localization, the decision threshold is the tunable knob, trainable on small data with augmentation — but the default per-pixel loss is **connectivity-blind**, so pair it with soft-clDice for gap-bridging. *Source:* [arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597].

**nnU-Net.** *Math:* no new operator — a standard U-Net whose hyperparameters are set by rule-based + empirical **self-configuration** over a dataset *fingerprint* (modality, spacing, shape, intensity histogram, class ratios), mapping fingerprint + GPU budget to patch/batch size, depth/pooling, resampling, normalization; 2D/3D/3D-cascade chosen by cross-validation. *Used for:* turnkey biomedical segmentation, SOTA across dozens of tasks with zero manual tuning. *How it works:* extract fingerprint → derive preprocessing + topology under hardware constraints → train (deep supervision, heavy augmentation, 5-fold CV) → auto-select best config/ensemble + connected-component postprocessing. *Hyphae relevance:* the strongest **small-data, no-tuning** path to a binary mask — auto-derives patch size and class-balanced sampling for the agar-plate regime; component postprocessing prunes agar-grain FPs, though connectivity still needs a topology loss. *Source:* [nature.com/articles/s41592-020-01008-z].

**Attention U-Net.** *Math:* U-Net + **attention gates** on skips: for encoder skip x and decoder gating g, α=σ₂(ψᵀ(σ₁(W_x x+W_g g+b))) (additive attention, α∈[0,1]); reweight x̂=α·x before concatenation, suppressing background. *Used for:* small/variable targets in cluttered medical scans where most pixels are background. *How it works:* standard encode/decode → at each skip score spatial relevance to decoder context → gate-multiply, concatenate, decode → threshold. *Hyphae relevance:* AGs **boost low-SNR thin ridges** by up-weighting sparse hyphal pixels against the dominant agar background — learned soft attention to filamentous structure that counters severe **FG/BG imbalance**; no extra labels. *Source:* [arxiv.org/abs/1804.03999].

**DeepCrack.** *Math:* SegNet-style encoder–decoder with **deep supervision** — side outputs at each of 5 scales, encoder/decoder features pairwise fused, total loss L=Σ_s w_s·BCE(side_s, y)+BCE(fused, y). *Used for:* pixel-level crack segmentation on pavement/concrete — thin, low-contrast, branching networks (near-isomorphic to hyphae). *How it works:* encode through SegNet → per-scale fuse encoder+decoder into a side-output crack map → deeply supervise every side output → fuse all scales (F>0.87). *Hyphae relevance:* purpose-built for **thin branching ridge networks under low contrast** — multi-scale fusion captures fine hyphal tips (large maps) and holistic topology (small maps), deep supervision combats the line class imbalance; the most directly transferable learned architecture. *Source:* [yhlleo.github.io/papers/DeepCrack-Neurocomputing2019.pdf].

**Tversky / Focal loss.** *Math:* Tversky index TI=TP/(TP+α·FP+β·FN), loss 1−TI (β>α penalizes FNs); Focal Tversky (1−TI)^γ focuses on hard/rare regions; focal loss FL=−α_t(1−p_t)^γ log(p_t) down-weights easy abundant background. *Used for:* training under extreme class imbalance (tiny lesions, vessels) where Dice/CE collapse to background. *How it works:* compute soft TP/FP/FN (or per-pixel p_t) → apply asymmetric weighting (β≫α) and/or focal exponent γ → backprop, emphasizing missed foreground / hard pixels. *Hyphae relevance:* directly targets the **sparse-foreground imbalance** — set β>α to recover faint low-SNR branches (FNs) that per-pixel losses drop; γ and β are the tunable knobs; complements (not replaces) soft-clDice's topology term. *Source:* [arxiv.org/abs/1706.05721], [arxiv.org/abs/1810.07842].

**Bbox-crop pre-stage.** *Math:* a two-stage cascade — Stage 1 a detector/coarse localizer (region proposal, blob/coarse seg, or low-res U-Net) producing boxes b=(x,y,w,h); Stage 2 runs fine segmentation only on the cropped, optionally upsampled patch x[b], raising the foreground pixel ratio. *Used for:* high-res segmentation of small objects in large images (medical ROI cropping, nnU-Net 3D-cascade). *How it works:* coarse-localize ROIs → crop (and upscale) → run the fine segmenter at high effective resolution → stitch back. *Hyphae relevance:* crops the plate to colony/mycelium regions, **mitigating extreme background imbalance** and letting the fine net see thin hyphae at higher resolution (better low-SNR recovery); also removes CA/agar-grain confounders at the plate margin; crop margin / detection threshold is a knob. *Source:* [nature.com/articles/s41592-020-01008-z], [arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597].

### 2.8 Topology / Centerline-Aware Losses & Tracing Nets

**clDice.** *Math:* soft skeletons S_P=skel(V_P), S_L=skel(V_L) via iterative soft min/max-pool morphology; Topology Precision T_prec=|S_P∩V_L|/|S_P|, Topology Sensitivity T_sens=|S_L∩V_P|/|S_L|; clDice=2·(T_prec·T_sens)/(T_prec+T_sens); used as L=(1−α)L_Dice+α(1−clDice). *Used for:* vessel/road/neuron/tubular segmentation where connectivity matters more than per-pixel overlap. *How it works:* differentiably skeletonize both masks → measure each skeleton inside the opposite mask → harmonic-mean into a connectivity score → blend with Dice, backprop. *Hyphae relevance:* strong junction/connectivity preservation with a formal **homotopy-equivalence guarantee** and a single knob α; drop-in differentiable loss for small-data fine-tuning — but ~88% added training time / ~52% VRAM over plain nnU-Net. *Source:* [10.1109/cvpr46437.2021.01629].

**Skeleton Recall Loss.** *Math:* GT skeleton S_L (CPU morphological thinning), dilate with a radius-2 diamond to a tubular region T, apply soft recall L_SR=1−(Σ_T p·g)/(Σ_T g). *Used for:* topology-preserving thin-structure segmentation (vessels, roads, cracks), a cheap drop-in over nnU-Net. *How it works:* skeletonize GT on CPU → dilate to a tubular band → compute soft recall of prediction inside that band → add to the base loss. *Hyphae relevance:* the **best efficiency/connectivity trade** — preserves centerline topology adding only ~8% training time vs clDice's ~88% (overhead cut >90% by moving topology compute to CPU); ideal for small-data hyphae training with tight GPU budget. *Source:* [arxiv.org/abs/2404.03010].

**Persistent-homology / Betti-matching loss.** *Math:* build a filtration (sublevel sets of the likelihood map), extract the persistence diagram (β0 components, β1 loops, with birth/death pairs); topology loss matches predicted vs GT diagrams, L_topo=Σ_i(persistence of unmatched/critical features)², penalizing wrong Betti numbers; persistence images vectorize the diagram. *Used for:* enforcing correct global topology (no spurious breaks/merges) in vessel/membrane/curvilinear seg. *How it works:* compute persistence diagrams of prediction and GT → optimally match topological features → penalize unmatched/short-lived features → backprop through critical-pixel gradients. *Hyphae relevance:* directly targets junction-gap/topology errors — embedding persistence images cuts Betti error and fragmentation; the gap-bridging fit for dense-overlap hyphae, but cubic cost and many spurious critical points on noisy likelihood maps make it the most expensive option. *Source:* [arxiv.org/html/2601.18045].

**Centerline-Dice variants (incl. Homotopy Warping).** *Math:* a family extending clDice — soft-skeleton variants swap the skeletonization (distance-transform-weighted or erosion centerlines) and reweight T_prec/T_sens by local radius. The notable cousin, **Homotopy Warping loss**, computes per-pixel topological criticality by warping predicted binary mask → GT (and vice versa) through topology-preserving *simple-point* flips, penalizing only flipped (critical) pixels: L_warp=Σ_{x∈C}BCE(p_x,g_x), found in O(n) via distance transform. *Used for:* connectivity-aware vessel/curvilinear segmentation driven by centerline alignment or critical-connection repair. *How it works:* derive a centerline/skeleton or warp the mask toward GT → identify topologically decisive pixels (skeleton overlap or simple-point flips) → apply a focused recall/BCE penalty there → blend with overlap loss. *Hyphae relevance:* operates on the *binary mask* (not the noisy likelihood), avoiding persistent-homology's irrelevant critical points at O(n) cost; on DRIVE, Warping loss beats clDice on Betti error (0.812 vs 1.218) and Dice (0.781 vs 0.749) — a strong, cheaper gap-fixing alternative for low-SNR hyphae. *Source:* [proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/98143953a7fd1319175b491888fc8df5-Paper-Conference.pdf].

**Orientation-aware iterative tracing nets.** *Math:* predict a sequence of moves along the centerline — each step regresses/classifies the next direction from a local patch (softmax over discretized angles or a von Mises head) + step length, conditioned on structure tensor / tangent: θ_{t+1}=f_φ(patch(x_t), θ_t), x_{t+1}=x_t+ℓ(cosθ_{t+1}, sinθ_{t+1}); branch points spawn new tracers (Deep Vessel Tracking / iCafe / RNN-tracer / deep-RL tracing). *Used for:* vessel/neuron/axon/road centerline extraction as a graph of connected curves. *How it works:* seed at endpoints/high-evidence pixels → crop a local oriented patch, predict next direction + stop probability → step forward, integrating evidence along the line → repeat, branching at junctions, until stop — yielding an explicit connected graph. *Hyphae relevance:* inherently low-SNR-robust (integrates along a traced line like the Dijkstra baseline) and naturally **bridges gaps and represents junctions/branching as graph topology** rather than fighting Frangi-style suppression; the move budget/step-length is the single knob, and tracing can run training-free from the phase-congruency evidence map. *Source:* [arxiv.org/abs/1806.06236].

### 2.9 Foundation Segmenters (SAM/SAM2) & Small-Data Transfer

**SAM.** *Math:* promptable mask decoder over a frozen ViT image embedding; a two-way transformer cross-attends learnable output/mask tokens against image embedding F + prompt embeddings (point/box positional encodings), emitting masks M=upsample(F)·MLP(token) with an IoU head; loss = focal+Dice; intensity/region-driven, no curvilinear/topology prior. *Used for:* zero-shot promptable segmentation of "anything." *How it works:* heavy ViT-H encodes the image once → encode a point/box/mask prompt → lightweight decoder fuses prompt+image tokens to 3 masks + IoU scores in real time → automatic mode tiles a point grid. *Hyphae relevance:* poor out-of-box — trained on blobby natural objects, it produces a clean mask but **fragments faint thin branching ridges** and ignores junction topology; training-free only as a coarse prompt, the foundation to fine-tune rather than deploy. *Source:* [arxiv.org/abs/2304.02643].

**SAM2 decoder-only fine-tune.** *Math:* same promptable objective (focal+Dice) on the SAM2 hiera encoder, but encoder + prompt encoder **frozen** and only decoder weights θ_dec updated — parameter delta is just the decoder head. *Used for:* small-data domain transfer to specialized imagery (tissue, cells, microscopy) with tens–hundreds of masks; single-notebook, no cluster. *How it works:* freeze encoder + prompt encoder → feed a small labeled set with point/box prompts → backprop focal+Dice through the decoder only → infer with the same prompting interface, now domain-tuned. *Hyphae relevance:* strong small-data / binary-mask / single-knob fit (prompt + decoder-only training); the documented failure mode is exactly the hyphae regime — accuracy holds on thick structure but **degrades on finest branches and junction gaps**. *Source:* [biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.08.687405v1.full].

**Medical SAM3.** *Math:* **full** fine-tune of all SAM3 parameters + a text-driven semantic-alignment objective — text embeddings t act as discriminative spatial queries q=align(t) resolved to masks, enabling Promptable Concept Segmentation from open-vocabulary text alongside point/box prompts; trained on 33 datasets / 10 modalities (~77k images, ~264k masks). *Used for:* universal prompt-driven medical segmentation where a *text* label ("vessel","lesion") replaces spatial prompts. *How it works:* aggregate heterogeneous medical corpora with mask+text pairs → fully fine-tune encoder+decoder+text path → semantic-alignment loss makes text embeddings spatial queries → at inference a text prompt yields the mask with no clicking. *Hyphae relevance:* attractive for training-free *semantic* prompting ("hyphae"/"mycelium" as text) and a clean mask, but it carries **no explicit thin-ridge or junction-connectivity prior** — low-SNR faint filaments and overlap gaps remain unaddressed without domain fine-tuning; heaviest compute of the family. *Source:* [arxiv.org/abs/2601.10880].

**MedSAM.** *Math:* SAM fine-tuned with **box prompt only** — encoders frozen, mask decoder updated under focal+Dice over 1.57M medical image–mask pairs (10 modalities, 30+ cancer types); the box b=(x₁,y₁,x₂,y₂) is encoded as positional embeddings, replacing point ambiguity with a deterministic region constraint. *Used for:* box-prompted segmentation of organs/lesions/tumors across CT/MRI/path/endoscopy. *How it works:* freeze encoders → train decoder on millions of (image, box, mask) triples → at inference draw one box → decoder emits a single binary mask within it. *Hyphae relevance:* one box is a poor container for a sprawling space-filling hyphal network (≈ whole plate), and like SAM it lacks a curvilinear/topology prior — useful as a region gate or warm start, weak on low-SNR thin branches and junction gaps; the box itself is a clean single knob. *Source:* [nature.com/articles/s41467-024-44824-z].

**Few-shot transfer adaptation.** *Math:* parameter-efficient fine-tuning of a frozen backbone — **LoRA** injects low-rank updates W'=W+BA (B∈R^{d×r}, A∈R^{r×d}, r≪d, <5% of params) into the hiera encoder + decoder; or prompt-tuning (TPNet) learns tubular priors via tunable encoding/decoding nets with a Morphology-Driven Region-Growing module exploiting tubular connectivity; loss commonly BCE+soft-Dice+Focal-Tversky, generally *without* an explicit clDice/persistent-homology term. *Used for:* adapting SAM/SAM2 to thin curvilinear targets (retinal vessels) and limited-annotation domains without training from scratch. *How it works:* freeze the backbone → insert LoRA adapters or tunable prompt/decoder heads (+connectivity module) → train <5% of params on a small labeled set with imbalance-aware loss → infer a mask leveraging learned tubular priors. *Hyphae relevance:* the **best-matched route** — small-data transfer + thin-structure + binary-mask + cheap-tuning, with TPNet's MDRG / VesselSAM explicitly targeting low-contrast ambiguous thin structures and junction connectivity; residual weakness is the finest branches unless a topology/centerline-aware loss (clDice/persistent homology) is added. *Source:* [arxiv.org/html/2510.10288v1]; TPNet [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41650434/]; VesselSAM [arxiv.org/html/2502.18185v1].

### 2.10 SOTA Curvilinear Benchmarks

**DRIVE / STARE retinal.** *Math:* per-pixel binary vessel mask vs expert tracings; classical baselines use multiscale Hessian vesselness (Frangi/Sato) or 2D matched filters / log-Gabor banks; modern SOTA is a U-Net-family CNN minimizing BCE/soft-Dice, increasingly with a topology term (soft-clDice). *Used for:* retinal fundus vessel segmentation (DRIVE 40 / STARE 20 images) — the canonical small-data thin-curvilinear benchmark. *How it works:* green-channel/contrast normalization → multiscale ridge filtering or a learned encoder–decoder produces a probability map → hysteresis or learned threshold → optional skeleton/topology loss recovers thin distal vessels. *Hyphae relevance:* the **closest analogue** — thin oriented ridges, very small training sets, Hessian/log-Gabor energy is exactly your phase-congruency baseline; clDice on DRIVE reduced β1 error 1.455→1.389 (gap-bridging). *Source:* [isi.uu.nl/Research/Databases/DRIVE/], [researchgate.net/publication/355885480].

**Massachusetts Roads.** *Math:* binary road-mask labeling over large aerial tiles; loss = pixel CE/Dice with connectivity-aware variants (soft-clDice on skeleta, or junction-F1 "Opt-J"); geometry via FCN/U-Net or orientation-aware modules. *Used for:* road-network extraction from aerial/satellite imagery (~1100 1500×1500 tiles) — the standard large-scale, dense-network, junction-heavy benchmark. *How it works:* tile+augment large RGB scenes → deep encoder–decoder predicts road probability → threshold → topology loss / graph post-processing reconnects occluded segments at intersections. *Hyphae relevance:* the **best proxy for the dense-overlap/junction-gap regime** — roads occlude and cross like hyphae; soft-clDice raised junction F1 0.881→0.916, and gap-bridging over occluders mirrors bridging agar-grain/aberration breaks. *Source:* [cs.toronto.edu/~vmnih/data/], [researchgate.net/publication/355885480].

**DIADEM neurite.** *Math:* not a pixel metric — a graph/tree-matching score; the DIADEM metric matches bifurcation/termination nodes between candidate and gold trees within a cylindrical search radius, weighting by topology, returning a 0–1 similarity. *Used for:* benchmarking automated neuron/neurite tracing in 3D light microscopy — the canonical branching-tree reconstruction challenge. *How it works:* trace → centerline tree (SWC) → align candidate to gold → search a cylinder around each gold critical point for a candidate node → score by matched bifurcations/terminations with topology penalties. *Hyphae relevance:* directly relevant to **junction/topology evaluation** — measures whether branch points and connectivity (not pixels) are right, the property Frangi/Sato destroy and your Dijkstra tracer must preserve; motivates centerline/graph scoring over IoU. *Source:* [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4339018/], [diademchallenge.org].

**TopCoW.** *Math:* voxel-level 3D vessel segmentation scored with topology-aware metrics — Dice + centerline-Dice (clDice) + component-detection F1 + Betti-number error — over 13 Circle-of-Willis classes; SOTA = nnU-Net-style 3D CNNs with multi-class topology losses (Skeleton Recall) where clDice fails to scale. *Used for:* topology-aware anatomical segmentation of the Circle of Willis from CTA/MRA (200 paired scans). *How it works:* 3D angiographic volume → CNN predicts per-voxel vessel/class labels → topology metrics (clDice, Betti) score connectivity → graph/component analysis classifies CoW variants. *Hyphae relevance:* demonstrates the topology-over-pixels loss stack you want; notably **clDice ran out of memory on the 13-class A100-40GB task while Skeleton Recall scaled** — a direct warning if you go multi-class/3D on hyphae. *Source:* [arxiv.org/abs/2312.17670], [arxiv.org/html/2404.03010v1].

**Topology metrics (Betti, clDice).** *Math:* **Betti numbers** βk from persistent homology (β0 components, β1 loops); "Betti error" = |β_pred−β_gt|; **Betti matching** matches predicted/GT persistence barcodes so topology is scored with spatial correspondence. **clDice** = harmonic mean of topology precision/recall on the intersection of each mask with its morphological skeleton; soft-clDice uses a differentiable soft-skeleton for training. *Used for:* evaluation metric *and* loss for any tubular/curvilinear segmentation where connectivity beats per-pixel overlap. *How it works:* skeletonize prediction and GT (soft, iterated min/max-pool for the loss) → measure each skeleton inside the other's mask (clDice) or build persistence diagrams and match bars (Betti) → penalize broken/extra components and missing loops → backprop (soft-clDice) or report (Betti error); Skeleton Recall replaces the GPU soft-skeleton with a precomputed CPU tubed skeleton. *Hyphae relevance:* the **core of the gap-bridging/junction objective** — clDice guarantees topology preservation up to homotopy equivalence, recovers FN gaps plain Dice misses, single differentiable add-on knob; Skeleton Recall is the cheap, training-light variant for small-data hyphal masks. *Source:* [researchgate.net/publication/355885480], [arxiv.org/html/2407.04683], [arxiv.org/html/2404.03010v1].

### 2.11 Astronomy: Streaks, Cosmic-Web & ISM Filaments

**DisPerSE.** *Math:* discrete Morse theory + persistent topology on the (smoothed) density field treated as a Morse function; compute the discrete gradient and Morse–Smale complex, where filaments are the *ascending 1-manifolds* connecting saddles to maxima; significance via **persistence** (death−birth) of critical-point pairs, thresholded at Nσ. *Used for:* tracing the cosmic web and 2D ISM/molecular-cloud filament networks from column-density maps. *How it works:* build the Morse–Smale complex from the discrete gradient → pair critical points and compute persistence → cancel low-persistence pairs below Nσ (topological denoising) → keep ascending manifolds (2-saddles→maxima) as the skeleton. *Hyphae relevance:* connectivity-first and **junction-aware** — natively traces a faint extended *branching network* and identifies junction/saddle points, persistence as the single knob; strong for dense-overlap topology that Frangi/Sato suppress. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2966.2011.18394.x], [doi.org/10.3390/galaxies14030053].

**FilFinder.** *Math:* morphological + intensity segmentation → **medial-axis skeletonization** → graph pruning; an adaptive (arctan-transform local) threshold mask isolates bright structure, reduced to a 1-px skeleton, converted to a graph whose branches are pruned by length/intensity, each spine fit with a transverse (Gaussian/Plummer) radial profile for width. *Used for:* characterizing filaments in Herschel dust-continuum maps — skeletons, lengths, widths, orientations. *How it works:* adaptive-threshold mask → medial-axis skeletonize → build skeleton graph, prune short/faint spurs → fit transverse radial profiles for width and position angle. *Hyphae relevance:* produces exactly the **binary-mask + skeleton + branch graph** deliverable, intensity-threshold and branch-length as knobs — but its intensity-mask front end is weaker at low SNR than orientation/phase methods, so best paired with a phase-congruency mask upstream. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/799/1/27], [doi.org/10.3390/galaxies14030053].

**getsf / getfilaments.** *Math:* **multi-scale spatial decomposition** into single-scale (LoG-like bandpass) planes, then *separation of structural components* (anisotropic single-scale planes flag elongated filament vs round source); detection images are **flattened** so residual background+noise has uniform variance, enabling a uniform Nσ cut. *Used for:* simultaneously extracting compact sources (cores) and filaments from multiwavelength Herschel maps over complex backgrounds. *How it works:* decompose into bandpass planes → at each scale separate filament vs source and subtract backgrounds → flatten the combined detection image to uniform noise → threshold at Nσ, track across scales, measure/mask. *Hyphae relevance:* scale-explicit filament/source separation + noise-flattening gives a principled **low-SNR detection threshold (single Nσ knob)** and clean masks at native ridge width — directly portable to thin hyphal ridges of varying caliber. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202039913], [doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201323043].

**Rolling Hough Transform (ISM).** *Math:* local oriented-evidence accumulation — binarize via unsharp mask (subtract disk-smoothed, threshold), then within a circular window of diameter D_W a Hough transform R(θ,x,y) accumulates how much on-mask power lies along each θ; thresholded by fraction Z. *Used for:* measuring orientation of faint diffuse linear structure in HI/ISM maps (HI fibers aligned with the magnetic field). *How it works:* unsharp-mask + bit-threshold → per-pixel windowed Hough over θ → keep angles above Z → emit a per-pixel position-angle field (optionally backproject to a mask). *Hyphae relevance:* integrates oriented evidence along a line so it recovers **both bright and faint** thin structures at low SNR while emitting an orientation map — a strong low-SNR ridge front end; D_W and Z are the knobs. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/789/1/82], [doi.org/10.3390/galaxies14030053].

**OCCULT-2 coronal-loop tracing.** *Math:* **oriented matched filter** + bandpass + ridge-following; bandpass = lowpass boxcar (nsm1) − highpass boxcar (nsm2); a Gaussian cross-section template rotated over 0–180° sets the local ridge direction by max-response angle; loops are traced by curvature-limited path following from flux maxima. *Used for:* automated tracing of curved coronal loops (and fibrils/streaks) in solar EUV/Hα. *How it works:* bandpass-filter → at each maximum rotate the Gaussian matched filter, pick peak-response orientation → step forward along it with a max-curvature constraint, re-estimating orientation → terminate at flux/curvature cutoffs, repeat. *Hyphae relevance:* unsupervised/**training-free**, models a Gaussian ridge cross-section (matched-filter SNR boost), and follows *curved* paths with explicit gap/curvature tolerance — a natural **gap-bridging** tracer; loop width σ and curvature/gap limits are the knobs. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1007/s11207-013-0269-9], [academic.oup.com/mnras/article/490/4/5567/5625861].

**STREAMFINDER / streak detection (LSD).** *Math:* two paradigms — **STREAMFINDER** is a model-fitting friends-of-friends in orbit space (for each star integrate trial orbits and count neighbors consistent in proper motion/photometry/sampled RV → per-star significance); **image-domain streak detection** uses parameter-free LSD (per-pixel level-line angle field → region-grow orientation-coherent line-support regions → rectangle fit → a-contrario NFA≤ε≈1 test). *Used for:* ultra-faint stellar streams in Gaia; satellite trails/cosmic rays/thin straight streaks in survey images. *How it works (LSD):* level-line angle field → region-grow into orientation-coherent regions → fit a rectangle → accept if NFA≤ε (auto false-alarm control, no tuning). *Hyphae relevance:* LSD is **training-free and parameter-free** with an automatic statistical (a-contrario) threshold — but only finds locally *straight* segments and models no junctions/curves, so it best serves as a per-segment ridge primitive whose pieces are linked/gap-bridged by a downstream graph (e.g. Dijkstra). *Source:* [doi.org/10.1093/mnras/sty912], [ipol.im/pub/art/2012/gjmr-lsd/article.pdf].

### 2.12 Biomedical Analogs & Curvilinear Tracking

**Retinal vessel segmentation (adversarial).** *Math:* multiscale Hessian vesselness (Frangi/Sato: V from eigenvalues, R_B=|λ1|/|λ2|, S=√(λ1²+λ2²)); modern deep variants add an adversarial "error discrimination network" D predicting where segmenter S is wrong, with S trained to minimize D's detected errors. *Used for:* fundus/OCT-A vasculature segmentation and AV classification. *How it works:* Hessian vesselness over scales (max response) → optionally feed into a U-Net → train S adversarially against D with synthetic thin-vessel error samples → threshold, then reconnect components (VNR post-processing) for a single connected structure. *Hyphae relevance:* the closest analog — same thin-ridge binary-mask target, with topology/connectivity metrics (Conn/Inf/Cor) and a training-free reconnection stage mirroring the baseline's Dijkstra gap-bridging; Frangi's known junction-suppression is the failure mode to design around. *Source:* [10.1002/mp.15627], [openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023W/CVAMD/papers/Dulau_Ensuring_a_Connected_Structure_for_Retinal_Vessels_Deep-Learning_Segmentation_ICCVW_2023_paper.pdf].

**Neurite tracing (APP2 / DIADEM).** *Math:* geodesic / least-cost path on a graph — cost c(x)=exp(α(1−I(x)/I_max)) (bright-on-dark) or a Hessian-derived ridge cost; compute the grey-weighted distance transform (GWDT) and extract the minimum-spanning-tree / shortest-path skeleton via fast-marching or Dijkstra; pruning by coverage/redundancy. *Used for:* reconstructing single-neuron axon/dendrite morphology from 3D light-microscopy (DIADEM/BigNeuron). *How it works:* pick a seed/soma → compute GWDT so bright tubules become low-cost → fast-marching builds an over-connected tree to all foreground voxels → prune by coverage (APP2) to the minimal tree explaining the signal. *Hyphae relevance:* branching-tree topology with **explicit junctions** and a single cost/pruning knob; the GWDT-then-shortest-path recipe is a direct template for **gap-bridging across low-SNR breaks while preserving branch points** — and is essentially the baseline's Dijkstra stage. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btt170].

**Diffusion-MRI tractography.** *Math:* per-voxel orientation model — diffusion tensor D (eigenvectors e1≥e2≥e3, FA) or higher-order fiber orientation distribution f(θ,φ) from spherical deconvolution; streamlines integrate dr/ds=v(r) following the principal direction, or probabilistic/global tractography maximizes a path likelihood. *Used for:* reconstructing white-matter fiber bundles and structural connectomes from dMRI. *How it works:* fit tensor/FOD per voxel → seed points → step along the dominant orientation with curvature/FA stopping → (probabilistic) sample many streamlines and accumulate, or (global) optimize all tracts jointly. *Hyphae relevance:* the **orientation-field-following** idea maps to oriented hyphal ridges; integrating directional evidence along a line is exactly the low-SNR remedy, and **crossing-fiber FOD models** are the analog for resolving dense-overlap junctions that a single-orientation model collapses. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.06.081].

**Guidewire/catheter detection (HessianNet).** *Math:* a Hessian-enhancement module computes 2nd-order-gradient eigenvalues λ1,λ2 → a vesselness-like contrast map concatenated into a CNN; the task is per-pixel semantic segmentation (U-Net / U-Net+Transformer / SegFormer), with a detector first emitting a bbox to crop the wire and reduce imbalance. *Used for:* real-time guidewire/catheter and tip localization in interventional X-ray fluoroscopy. *How it works:* detect a bbox around the wire to crop → compute Hessian λ1,λ2 enhancement of the thin low-contrast structure → concatenate enhancement with raw input into a segmentation CNN → output binary wire mask (HessianNet Dice 0.8990 vs plain U-Net 0.8890). *Hyphae relevance:* direct demonstration that classical Hessian ridge enhancement boosts a learned thin-structure segmenter under low contrast, plus the **bbox-crop trick to fight extreme thin-foreground imbalance** — both portable to hyphae as a training-free front-end with a small-data CNN. *Source:* [arxiv.org/html/2404.08805v1], [link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11548-026-03647-7], [10.1016/j.cmpb.2020.105420].

**Needle detection in ultrasound.** *Math:* oriented line/ridge integration — Radon/Hough accumulates intensity along candidate lines (peak ⇒ needle axis), often on a Frangi/Gabor-enhanced image; the structure tensor T=Gσ*(∇I∇Iᵀ) gives dominant orientation (coherence (μ1−μ2)/(μ1+μ2)) used to gate and steer the line search, integrating evidence along the assumed insertion direction to lift the needle out of speckle. *Used for:* localizing biopsy/anesthesia needles (and tip) in 2D/3D B-mode ultrasound under heavy speckle. *How it works:* enhance tubular response (Frangi/Gabor) → estimate orientation via structure tensor or restrict Hough to the known insertion angle → accumulate evidence along candidate lines (Radon/Hough/projection) → localize the tip. *Hyphae relevance:* the canonical **"integrate evidence along a line to beat low SNR"** recipe and structure-tensor orientation gating map directly onto faint hyphal filaments against agar speckle; the global-line assumption is the limitation for curved/branching hyphae, motivating piecewise/curvilinear extensions. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2017.06.012].

### 2.13 Particle-Physics & Seismic Tracking (Gap-Bridging)

**Kalman filter track finding.** *Math:* recursive linear estimator over a state x_k (position, slope/direction, curvature) along an ordered index k — predict x_k⁻=F_k x_{k−1}, P_k⁻=F_k P_{k−1}F_kᵀ+Q_k (process noise Q = allowed turning/scattering); update with m_k: x_k=x_k⁻+K_k(m_k−H_k x_k⁻), gain K_k=P_k⁻H_kᵀ(H_k P_k⁻H_kᵀ+R_k)⁻¹; P_k propagates a predicted search window. *Used for:* charged-particle track reconstruction at LHC/HEP — the dominant production method (CMS/ATLAS). *How it works:* seed from a hit triplet → predict the next hit location + error ellipse → pick the compatible hit in the gate, update (or skip the layer, inflating P, to bridge a missing hit) → iterate to track end, optionally smooth backward. *Hyphae relevance:* strong **gap-bridging** — the predict-with-inflated-covariance step extends a hypha across a low-SNR break by extrapolating direction/curvature; the gate is the single knob; training-free and incremental along the filament. *Source:* [arxiv.org/abs/1601.08245], [10.1016/0168-9002(87)90887-4].

**Cellular-automaton track finder.** *Math:* discrete CA on a graph of candidate short segments (cells = neighbor-hit links); each cell's state increments iff a compatible-direction predecessor of state n exists: state(c)←n+1; iterated to a fixed point, state = length of the longest collinear chain ending there; track candidates = back-traced high-state chains. *Used for:* fast parallel pattern recognition / segment-linking in HEP trackers (CATS, CMS CA seeding), feeding a Kalman fit. *How it works:* build cells from locally compatible hit/segment pairs (angle + distance cuts) → sweep the CA rule synchronously so states grow along collinear sequences while noise stays low-state → threshold on state, back-trace high-state cells → hand to a fitter. *Hyphae relevance:* junction-gap friendly and inherently parallel — local collinearity rules grow ridge chains and naturally **enumerate branching alternatives at junctions** where Frangi/Sato collapse; the collinearity-angle tolerance is the knob, training-free. *Source:* [10.1016/S0168-9002(02)00790-8], [researchgate.net/publication/263049908].

**Hough-transform track finding.** *Math:* voting in a parametric accumulator — each point maps to a curve in parameter space; for ρ=x cosθ+y sinθ every pixel votes for all (ρ,θ) through it; accumulator peaks A(ρ,θ) = collinear sets (generalizable to circles/arcs/splines, or a conformal map for curved tracks). *Used for:* global seedless track/line finding (drift chambers, trigger-level pattern recognition) and classic image line/curve detection. *How it works:* extract candidate ridge/edge pixels → each votes into the (ρ,θ) (or curvature-extended) accumulator → find peaks above threshold = global line hypotheses → back-project to collect inliers. *Hyphae relevance:* excellent **low-SNR integration** — votes accumulate along a whole line so a faint broken hypha sums to a clear peak (bridges gaps globally); but assumes near-straight/parametric segments, so long curved hyphae need piecewise/curvature-extended voting; knob = peak threshold. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1016/0031-3203(81)90009-1], [arxiv.org/abs/1601.08245].

**Ant-colony optimization (ant-tracking).** *Math:* stigmergic graph search — ants move pixel→pixel with p_ij ∝ τ_ij^α·η_ij^β (pheromone τ accumulates on edges; heuristic η encodes ridge strength + direction-consistency); pheromone evaporates (τ←(1−ρ)τ) and is reinforced along good paths, so the colony converges to high-continuity curvilinear structures; output thinned (Zhang–Suen) to a 1-px line. *Used for:* automatic fault tracking in 3D seismic volumes — tracing/connecting amplitude-discontinuity features into continuous faults; joint denoising + connectivity enhancement. *How it works:* seed ants on ridge/discontinuity voxels → each walks following the local extension direction with bounded turning, depositing pheromone (direction-consistency suppresses noise) → evaporate + reinforce so continuous structures accumulate pheromone and gaps bridge → threshold pheromone + skeletonize. *Hyphae relevance:* direct match — **gap-bridging + low-SNR denoising in one pass**, direction-consistency handles oriented ridges and curvature, yields a clean binary/skeleton mask; knob = evaporation/direction-consistency bound; training-free. *Source:* [sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0098300412002804], [library.seg.org/doi/10.1190/tle40070502.1].

**FaultSeg3D.** *Math:* 3D encoder–decoder CNN (U-Net-style) trained on **synthetic** seismic volumes with known fault labels, minimizing a **balanced cross-entropy** loss (class-weighted for the extreme thin-structure imbalance) — a learned multi-scale ridge/discontinuity detector replacing hand-built coherence + ant-tracking. *Used for:* end-to-end fault segmentation in 3D seismic images, cleaner and more continuous than coherence/semblance + ant-tracking. *How it works:* generate large synthetic volumes (folding/faulting + noise, exact masks) → train the 3D U-Net with balanced CE → infer a fault-probability volume on real data → optionally threshold/thin. *Hyphae relevance:* a small-data path via **synthetic supervision** — train on simulated thin oriented branching structures for a continuous mask-ready map robust to low SNR and junctions; departs from "training-free" but the threshold is the single knob and the synthetic-data recipe transfers to hyphae. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1190/geo2018-0646.1].

### 2.14 Cytoskeleton Filament Extraction (Closest Twin)

**SOAX (Stretching Open Active Contours).** *Math:* each filament is an open curve r(s) minimizing E=∫[½(α|r′|²+β|r″|²)+E_img(r)+E_str(r)]ds; internal tension α / bending β enforce smoothness; **image energy** = negative Gaussian-smoothed intensity (gradient toward the bright center); **stretching force** k_str·t̂ applied only at the two endpoints, growing the contour along its tangent until image energy stops increasing; ridge points (gradient sign change) seed the SOACs. *Used for:* 2D/3D extraction of biopolymer/cytoskeletal networks — actin, microtubules, septin, collagen — from fluorescence/confocal stacks. *How it works:* detect ridge seed points → initialize short SOACs → evolve under image+stretch+smoothing, stretching at tips to span the filament → resolve collisions into T-junctions, cluster into network junctions, cut/splice SOACs to pass smoothly through crossings. *Hyphae relevance:* strong junction-gap + low-SNR fit — line-integrated evidence bridges intensity gaps and **preserves crossings**; two key knobs (ridge threshold, stretch factor) tunable but it outputs centerlines (needs rasterize/dilate for a mask) and wants local SNR ≈ 5. *Source:* [nature.com/articles/srep09081], [10.1002/cm.20481].

**CT-FIRE (Curvelet Transform + FIbeR Extraction).** *Math:* (1) **discrete curvelet transform** — anisotropic needle-shaped basis tuned to curved edges; coefficient thresholding denoises/enhances oriented fiber edges. (2) **FIRE** — distance-transform map of the thresholded image, extract a nucleation-point/skeleton network, link points into smooth fiber paths with angle/length constraints. *Used for:* quantifying individual **collagen fibers** in SHG/H&E tissue (length, width, angle, straightness) from breast/tumor stroma — the canonical "collagen fingerprint." *How it works:* curvelet-denoise → threshold to a binary fiber map, compute distance transform → find nucleation points, extend fibers along maximal-distance ridges → trim/merge by length/orientation. *Hyphae relevance:* good oriented-ridge + tunable-knob fit (curvelet threshold) yielding per-fiber centerlines, but FIRE's distance-transform skeletonization is sensitive to gaps/low SNR and weaker at dense overlapping junctions than active contours. *Source:* [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4019006/].

**CurveAlign.** *Math:* built on the same discrete curvelet transform; each retained coefficient yields a position and a dominant orientation angle θ; alignment is quantified by circular statistics over {θ} — alignment coefficient and relative angle to a boundary (tumor margin) per curvelet center. *Used for:* tissue-scale collagen orientation/alignment (Tumor-Associated Collagen Signatures relative to a boundary); complements CT-FIRE. *How it works:* curvelet-transform, keep top coefficients → read each center + angle as a local fiber-orientation sample → optionally trace fibers (CT-FIRE mode), group curvelets → compute alignment/density and angle-to-boundary statistics/heatmaps. *Hyphae relevance:* excellent training-free **orientation/anisotropy** descriptor with one main knob (coefficient keep-fraction), but it is a quantifier of orientation, not a centerline tracer or mask segmenter — gap-bridging and junction topology are out of scope. *Source:* [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4675565/], [eliceirilab.org/software/curvealign/].

**SIFNE (Single-molecule-localization Filament Network Extraction).** *Math:* an oriented-filtered ridge map — a bank of oriented filters (steerable / oriented 2nd-derivative-of-Gaussian) gives per-pixel orientation and filament-likelihood; fragments are extracted as oriented line segments, then linked by a **fragment-pairing optimization** scoring candidate joins by proximity, orientation continuity, and curvature (a global graph/assignment over endpoints). *Used for:* extracting dense filament networks (microtubules, actin) from super-resolution (SMLM/STORM) and conventional fluorescence, where filaments are densely packed and cross. *How it works:* enhance with an oriented-filter bank for orientation + ridge response → extract short fragments → globally pair/link across gaps using orientation-continuity and geometric cost → reconstruct full filaments and junctions. *Hyphae relevance:* very strong **junction-gap** fit — the explicit fragment-pairing step is purpose-built to bridge gaps and reconnect across crossings under dense overlap; orientation filtering helps low SNR, though parameter count exceeds a single knob. *Source:* [nature.com/articles/nmeth.4206].

**TSOAX (Temporal SOAX).** *Math:* the same SOAC energy (tension α, bending β, Gaussian-image energy, endpoint stretch) extended with a **temporal correspondence / multi-object tracking** term linking SOACs across frames by minimizing a combined spatial-overlap + motion cost. *Used for:* extracting and tracking time-varying filament networks (actin/microtubule dynamics) from 2D/3D time-lapse movies. *How it works:* run SOAC extraction per frame → establish temporal correspondence by matching SOACs between consecutive frames under a motion+overlap cost → maintain consistent filament/junction identities, splice/merge across time → output time-resolved centerline networks. *Hyphae relevance:* inherits SOAX's junction-gap/low-SNR strengths (line-integrated evidence, T-junction resolution, SNR≈5) and would suit **time-lapse hyphal growth**, but the temporal machinery is wasted on single static plate images and it still emits centerlines, not a mask. *Source:* [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6368602/].

### 2.15 Cryo-EM Low-SNR Filament Picking

**STRIPER.** *Math:* oriented line-enhancing filter bank — convolve with anisotropic Gaussian kernels rotated over θ∈[0,π), take R(x)=max_θ(G_θ*I)(x); then Steger's ridge detector (Hessian H=[[I_xx,I_xy],[I_xy,I_yy]] at scale = filament width σ; eigenvector of largest-magnitude eigenvalue = line normal; sub-pixel ridge where the first directional derivative vanishes); hysteresis on the response links pixels into lines. *Used for:* tracing curvilinear structures — vasculature, roads, cytoskeletal filaments (the original target). *How it works:* smooth/enhance with rotated oriented-Gaussian kernels (max response integrates along the dominant orientation) → Steger sub-pixel ridge extraction at the filament-width scale → detect line crossings, mark the meeting point as a junction and split into two → link ridge pixels by hysteresis. Four parameters — filament width, mask width, upper/lower hysteresis thresholds — tuned by an included grid-search. *Hyphae relevance:* strong fit — training-free, **explicitly junction-preserving** (splits at crossings rather than suppressing them like Frangi/Sato), oriented-Gaussian preprocessing integrates evidence under low SNR, hysteresis gives a binary mask, and the four-parameter grid-search collapses toward the brief's single-knob ideal. *Source:* [biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.02.28.969196v1.full].

**crYOLO filament mode.** *Math:* YOLO-style single-shot CNN — the image is gridded S×S and a fully-convolutional net regresses, per cell, box coordinates + objectness p(object); YOLO multi-part loss (localization + confidence) on positive-only annotations. Filament mode fits a line/ridge through the picked centroids, then tiles boxes along the trace at a user spacing. *Used for:* automated particle picking in cryo-EM; filament mode for actin/microtubule/amyloid/helical assemblies. *How it works:* train (or use a general model) on a few positive boxes (~40 particles reaches AUC≈0.9, no negatives needed) → run the CNN to detect particles as points at low SNR (AUC>0.8 to noise level 6) → trace filaments through detected support points → place equidistant boxes along each trace. *Hyphae relevance:* small-data and low-SNR-robust, but **point-detection-then-trace** rather than a native mask, so it needs mask conversion and gives a sparse, spacing-quantized trace rather than dense junction topology. *Source:* [10.1038/s42003-019-0437-z], [10.1038/s42003-020-0790-y].

**Template cross-correlation picking.** *Math:* normalized cross-correlation NCC(u)=Σ_x(I(x)−Ī_u)(T(x−u)−T̄)/(σ_{I,u}σ_T) via FFT for all shifts u; peaks above threshold = detections. For filaments the template is a short line/segment patch and the search includes in-plane rotation, so the score field is an **oriented matched filter** (the Neyman-Pearson optimum for a known signal in additive Gaussian noise). *Used for:* classic cryo-EM picking (SIGNATURE, RELION autopick, EMAN) and template/matched-filter line detection generally. *How it works:* build template(s) — class average/projection/synthetic line patch → FFT-based normalized cross-correlation over positions and in-plane rotations → max over rotations for an orientation-marginalized score map → threshold + NMS (or hysteresis-link for filaments). *Hyphae relevance:* matched-filter integration along an oriented template is **near-optimal for low SNR** and training-free, but a fixed straight-template assumption handles curved/branching hyphae and junctions poorly and is sensitive to width/contrast mismatch (chromatic aberration, agar grain) without per-scale templates. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1016/j.jsb.2006.05.010], [doi.org/10.7554/eLife.42166].

**Topaz.** *Math:* positive-unlabeled (PU) CNN classifier/detector — a convolutional region classifier f(x) outputs p(particle), trained with a PU objective (GE-binomial / non-negative PU risk) that corrects for unlabeled pixels containing both positives and negatives given a class prior π; detections are score-map maxima above threshold; a separate U-Net denoiser (L2/L1-regressed) handles the low-SNR regime. *Used for:* particle picking in cryo-EM/cryo-ET from sparse positive labels; micrograph denoising. *How it works:* label a small set of positive particle centers → train the region classifier with the PU loss using the class prior π → slide the network for a dense particle-probability map → threshold + NMS (optionally denoise first with Topaz-Denoise). *Hyphae relevance:* excellent low-SNR + small-data fit (**PU learning + dedicated denoiser**, single effective knob = score threshold / prior), but like crYOLO it outputs particle points, not a connected branching mask, so junction topology and gap-bridging need a downstream tracing step. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0575-8], [doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-18952-1].

### 2.16 Remote Sensing & Materials Networks

**Road extraction (D-LinkNet).** *Math:* LinkNet (ResNet-34 encoder + transposed-conv decoder, skip-add links) + a stacked parallel **dilated-convolution** center block (dilation d∈{1,2,4,8}), giving receptive field r=1+Σ2(k−1)d_i without resolution loss; trained with combined BCE + soft-Dice on a sigmoid road map; binary mask via fixed threshold. *Used for:* aerial/satellite road network extraction (DeepGlobe 2018 winner). *How it works:* ResNet-34 encoder downsamples → cascaded dilated convolutions enlarge the receptive field to span long thin roads while preserving detail → decoder upsamples with additive skip links → sigmoid + threshold. *Hyphae relevance:* the dilated center directly targets **long, thin, low-contrast linear structures** and integrates evidence along the line (low-SNR fit), single threshold = one knob; but training-hungry and binary-mask only, weak on junction topology. *Source:* [openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018_workshops/papers/w4/Zhou_D-LinkNet_LinkNet_With_CVPR_2018_paper.pdf].

**River/drainage (D8 flow accumulation).** *Math:* steepest-descent flow routing on a DEM — flow direction = argmax over 8 neighbors of drop slope (z_center−z_neighbor)/distance (D8 single-flow); flow accumulation A(c)=number of upstream cells draining through c via topological accumulation; channels = {c : A(c)≥τ}. *Used for:* hydrology — drainage networks, stream channels, watersheds from DEMs. *How it works:* fill sinks/pits → assign each cell one of 8 directions by steepest descent → accumulate upstream contributing area along the direction graph → threshold accumulation to extract the connected channel tree. *Hyphae relevance:* treating a ridge-strength response as a "height field," D8 accumulation integrates evidence along oriented paths and yields an inherently **connected, branching tree** (junction/gap-bridging fit) with a single area threshold τ; training-free, but assumes a monotonic surface and a known source/outlet. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1016/0734-189X(84)90011-0].

**Crack detection (DeepCrack — remote-sensing variant).** *Math:* multi-scale hierarchical convolutional feature fusion on a SegNet/HED encoder-decoder with deep supervision; per-scale side outputs are upsampled and fused (learned 1×1 weighted sum); loss = class-balanced weighted CE with positive weight w1=C0/C1 (neg/pos ratio, cracks <5% of pixels) over all side outputs + the fused output; binary mask via a single fixed global threshold T≈0.75–0.91. *Used for:* pixel-wise binary segmentation of thin curvilinear pavement/concrete cracks; cross-dataset (CrackTree260, F>0.87 on three test sets). *How it works:* SegNet encoder extracts features at 5 scales → each emits a deeply-supervised side prediction → side maps fused to a single logit capturing line structures across scales → threshold the softmax, with classical post-processing (length/curvature) to repair dashed-line dropout. *Hyphae relevance:* purpose-built for thin, low-contrast, poorly-continuous lines — the exact low-SNR regime; class-balanced loss handles extreme thin-foreground imbalance; single threshold T = knob; but authors concede **gap dropout needs classical reconnection**, and it is training-hungry. *Source:* [10.1016/j.neucom.2019.01.036], [ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/8517148/].

**Fingerprint ridge-flow / orientation field.** *Math:* gradient-based structure tensor — orientation θ=½·atan2(2·ΣGx·Gy, Σ(Gx²−Gy²)) over a local window (dominant eigenvector of J=[[ΣGx²,ΣGxGy],[ΣGxGy,ΣGy²]]); ridge coherence (λ1−λ2)/(λ1+λ2); ridges enhanced by oriented (steerable/Gabor) filters tuned to θ and ridge frequency. *Used for:* fingerprint enhancement, orientation-field/ridge-frequency mapping, singular-point detection. *How it works:* per-pixel gradients → average the structure tensor over a block → derive dominant ridge orientation θ and coherence → apply a Gabor filter steered to θ and local frequency to amplify ridges → binarize. *Hyphae relevance:* the structure tensor gives per-pixel orientation that lets oriented filtering **integrate evidence along the hyphal axis** (low-SNR fit) and coherence flags well-defined ridges; training-free with block-window/frequency as the knob; junction/crossing points (low coherence) are the weak spot — exactly where Frangi/Sato also fail. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1109/34.244676], [10.1109/34.709565].

**Fiber detection.** *Math:* Hessian-based oriented ridge/tube filtering + tracing — at scale σ, Hessian eigenvalues λ1,λ2 (|λ1|≤|λ2|) and eigenvectors give ridge strength and orientation; a line response (Frangi V, or Steger's λ-based sub-pixel line points) is computed over multiple σ and max-pooled; fibers traced/linked along the eigenvector direction. *Used for:* detecting/quantifying fibers in materials science (composites, paper, collagen, nanofiber/SEM) and biological filament networks. *How it works:* multi-scale Gaussian smoothing matching fiber width → Hessian eigen-decomposition for ridge strength + orientation per scale → max-pool the response across scales → trace and link centerlines along the principal eigenvector, bridging small gaps by orientation continuity. *Hyphae relevance:* directly the geometric (2nd-derivative) ridge model the brief calls for — oriented, multi-scale, training-free, with σ / vesselness β,c as knobs; orientation-guided tracing bridges small gaps, but Frangi/Sato vesselness inherently suppresses junctions (the named failure mode), motivating Steger tracing or a topology-aware add-on. *Source:* [doi.org/10.1007/BFb0056195], [doi.org/10.1109/34.659930].

## 3. Best Fits for Fungal Hyphae

1. **STRIPER (oriented-Gaussian + Steger + hysteresis)** — the single most on-target classical pipeline: training-free, geometry-based, explicitly *splits at crossings* (junction-preserving where Frangi/Sato collapse), oriented preprocessing integrates evidence under low SNR, outputs a hysteresis binary mask, and its 4-knob grid-search collapses toward the brief's single-knob ideal.

2. **Tensor voting** — uniquely covers *both* hard regimes at once: the decaying voting field **bridges gaps** across faint/broken ridges (low-SNR) while the ball-tensor component **explicitly flags junctions/branches** that Hessian filters suppress; non-iterative, training-free, single neighborhood-scale knob.

3. **Jerman enhancement** — the drop-in upgrade to the Frangi baseline ridge channel: the eigenvalue-*ratio* with τ-regularization **reinforces response at bifurcations** instead of zeroing it, directly fixing the dense-overlap failure mode while keeping a single τ knob and training-free operation.

4. **APP2/GWDT least-cost tracing (with phase-congruency cost)** — formalizes and extends the baseline's Dijkstra step: a grey-weighted distance transform + shortest-path/MST builds a **branching tree with explicit junctions**, bridging low-SNR breaks while preserving branch points; one cost/pruning knob, training-free.

5. **Skeleton Recall Loss on an nnU-Net / few-shot SAM2 backbone** — the best small-data learned route to a connected binary mask: nnU-Net/LoRA-tuned SAM2 self-configures from a handful of labeled plates, and Skeleton Recall adds **centerline-topology supervision (gap-bridging/junctions)** at only ~8% training overhead vs clDice's ~88% — the cheap topology term that turns a connectivity-blind segmenter connectivity-aware.

6. **Coherence-enhancing diffusion + cross-channel fringe suppression (preprocessor pair)** — attacks the two named confounders before detection: coherence-enhancing diffusion smooths *along* hyphal orientation to **close interrupted lines** (gap-bridging) under a single coherence knob, while cross-channel/lateral-color correction strips the **chromatic-aberration pseudo-filaments** so the geometry detector never locks onto color fringes; both training-free.
