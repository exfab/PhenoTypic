CellProfiler · Zernike moments · plain-language primer

A radial "equalizer" for colony brightness

how a handful of numbers (moments) describe the center→edge brightness curve

A Zernike moment is one slider on a graphic equalizer. Just as an audio EQ summarizes a sound as "how much bass, mid, treble," Zernike summarizes a colony's brightness profile as "how much of each standard radial shape." No single moment is the whole picture — the set of sliders is the description, and stacking the shapes back up rebuilds the curve. Push the sliders below and watch it happen.

1 · The thing we're describing

Stand at a colony's center and walk to its edge, noting the brightness. You get a curve: brightness vs. distance from center (distance rescaled so center = 0, edge = 1). Different colonies trace different curves.

Dense center

Bright, packed middle that fades outward.

Bright rim

Faint center, growth concentrated at the edge.

Bullseye / rings

Alternating bright and dark concentric bands.

2 · The library of standard shapes

Zernike keeps a fixed set of "template curves" (the radial polynomials), ordered from smooth to wiggly. The index n is the slider's frequency: low n = broad trends, high n = fine concentric detail. These are the EQ bands — bass to treble for radius.

What just happened
Each slider you moved is one Zernike moment. The reconstruction is the templates added back up, each scaled by its moment — exactly how an equalizer rebuilds a sound from its bands. "Show the real recipe" computes each moment as the overlap between the target curve and that template (the projection CellProfiler does in code). A few sliders capture the gist; adding higher n sharpens the fine rings.

4 · Reading the first sliders on their own

Because the templates have fixed meanings, the first few moments are interpretable by themselves — no reconstruction needed.

M0 · average

The overall brightness level — the DC term. Big colony glow, nothing about shape.

M2 · center↔edge

A single signed number. − means center-heavy; + means edge-heavy. The dense-center colony scores strongly negative.

M4 · bullseye

How much bright-center / dark-ring / bright-rim (target) pattern is present. Lights up for concentric morphotypes.

5 · Two more things worth knowing

Where direction comes in

Everything above is purely radial (distance only). A second index, m, captures direction around the circle — lopsided or lobed patterns. Each m has its own smooth→wiggly radial ladder, so radius and angle are described together. Taking the moment's size (ignoring its orientation) makes the description rotation-proof.

m = 0uniform
m = 1one-sided
m = 2two-lobed

The one big catch

Zernike assumes the colony fills a circle — it draws the smallest enclosing circle and scores empty space as zero. Fine for round, solid colonies. But a sparse / branching colony leaves most of the circle empty, so the moments mostly report "lots of empty edge" instead of real internal structure. The math is right; it's just answering a less useful question.

solidwell described
sparsemostly empty

In one breath

The rings path describes the brightness curve by chopping it into a few concentric bins (like four loudness readings across a song). Zernike describes the same curve by its equalizer settings — how much of each standard radial shape. The first sliders already read as "center-heavy vs edge-heavy" (M2) and "bullseye-ness" (M4); stacking them all back up reconstructs the curve. Two languages, one radial brightness profile — CellProfiler simply reports both.
