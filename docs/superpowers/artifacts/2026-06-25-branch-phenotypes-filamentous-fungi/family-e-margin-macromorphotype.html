Margin complexity & macromorphotype

How rugged is the colony edge, and what gross growth form does the whole silhouette belong to? These descriptors read the boundary and overall shape of a mask — entire vs. ragged margins, compact vs. diffuse forms — without ever resolving a single hypha, which is why most survive dense, low-resolution growth.

The macromorphotype vocabulary

Family E sits where colloquial colony description meets computable geometry: a handful of edge categories and silhouette classes that biologists already use by eye, recast as mask-readable numbers.

Macroscopic margin type

Regime B🟢 B-robust

A categorical label for the colony edge — entire, undulate, lobate, filamentous or rhizoid — assigned from boundary shape. Compresses a continuum of margin ruggedness into the descriptive classes mycologists already use.

Convex-perimeter deficit / convexity

Regime B🟢 B-robust

The ratio of the convex-hull perimeter to the colony's actual perimeter. A smooth disc scores near 1; an indented, ragged margin makes the true perimeter much longer than the taut hull, driving convexity down — a direct read of how concave the edge is.

Inputbinary mask
Robustmedium
FromRoots
SourcePaul & Thomas 1998

Perimeter-to-area ratio

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Boundary length divided by enclosed area, P / A. A compact disc minimises edge per unit area; as the colony spreads filaments and frays its margin, perimeter outpaces area and the ratio climbs — a simple ruggedness index.

Robustmedium

Roughness / hairy-length

Regime B🟡 A-feasible

The length of filaments that protrude beyond a compact core boundary — the "hairiness" of the colony. Quantifies how far loose hyphae reach past the dense centre, but it needs those filaments to be individually resolved, so it is the fragile member of this family.

Pellet / clump / dispersed classification

Regime B🟢 B-robust

A rule- or ML-assigned macromorphology label — pellet, clump or dispersed — derived from a bundle of silhouette features (area, compactness, roughness, circularity). The standard submerged-fermentation classification, read almost entirely from shape.

Smooth-vs-hairy pellet class

Regime B🟢 B-robust

A two-way split among pellets, by thresholding edge roughness, the relative annular diameter (RAD) or compactness. A smooth pellet has a clean dense rim; a hairy pellet wears a loose fringe of outer filaments.

Reading concavity off the hull

The most threshold-robust descriptors in this family are the ones that compare the colony against its convex hull — concavity that never depends on resolving a single filament.

The macromorphology axis: compact → diffuse

Submerged-culture morphology lines up on one silhouette axis, from a tight pellet through a loose clump to a fully diffuse mycelium.

Smooth vs hairy: the outer-zone fraction

Within the pellet class, the split is the loose outer corona — the relative annular diameter (RAD) that separates a clean dense pellet from a fringed one.

Industrial fermentation reads morphology straight off the silhouette

Submerged-culture morphology assessment classifies pellet, clump and dispersed forms almost entirely from silhouette geometry — area, circularity, compactness, solidity and perimeter-to-area — because in stirred bioreactors those gross shape classes are what drive broth rheology and oxygen transfer. This is the same Regime-B logic PhenoTypic already applies to compact colonies, scaled up to whole growth forms.

The documented soft spot is the clump ↔ pellet ambiguity: a loose, slightly irregular pellet and a tight, rounded clump occupy overlapping regions of shape space, so the boundary between those two classes is the one a rule or classifier gets wrong first.

  • Most threshold-robust: the convex-hull comparisons — convex-perimeter deficit / convexity and perimeter-to-area ratio — and the pellet/clump/dispersed label that rests on them. None require resolving an individual filament, so they hold up under dense, low-resolution growth.
  • Most fragile: roughness / hairy-length (🟡) needs the protruding filaments actually resolved, and elevation / surface texture (🎨) needs shading or 3-D — both fail on a flat, overhead, low-resolution mask.
  • Reads-as-named caveat: the macroscopic margin type label is robust except for the rhizoid-vs-filamentous split, which needs genuine edge detail.
