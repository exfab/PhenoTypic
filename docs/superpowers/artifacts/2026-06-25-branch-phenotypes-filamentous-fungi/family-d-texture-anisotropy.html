Texture & anisotropy

How rough and how directional is the mycelium, read straight off the grayscale image? These descriptors quantify branching density and preferred growth direction from local pixel statistics and the spatial-frequency spectrum — no segmentation, no skeleton, no thresholded mask required.

GLCM / Haralick features

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Tally how often each pair of grey levels sits a fixed step apart into a grey-level co-occurrence matrix, then read summary numbers off it — contrast, homogeneity, energy, entropy, correlation. Fine, busy hyphal texture and smooth confluent lawn give very different signatures.

Inputgrayscale
FromMycology
SourceHaralick 1973

Directional GLCM anisotropy

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Recompute the same Haralick features for offsets at 0°, 45°, 90°, 135° and compare them. If a feature swings strongly with angle the texture is directional (aligned hyphae); if it is flat across angles the growth is isotropic.

FromMycology
SourceHaralick 1973

FFT / Fourier orientation & power spectrum

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Take the 2-D Fourier transform of the image and integrate its power spectrum by angle and by radius. The angular profile gives the preferred growth orientation; the radial profile gives coarseness (where the spatial-frequency energy concentrates).

Inputgrayscale
FromMycology
SourceOrientationJ

Gabor filter-bank energy

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Convolve the image with a bank of Gabor wavelets tuned to several orientations and scales, then collect the response energy in each channel. The channel that lights up reveals branch direction; the spread across scales reports fineness.

Local-orientation angle histogram

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Estimate the dominant orientation inside each small window, then pool all the windows into a histogram of angles. The shape of that histogram summarises the mix of shallow, medium and steep growth directions across the whole colony.

Growth-direction order parameter / anisotropy

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Pool the local growth directions (via the image structure tensor) into one number: how concentrated they are about a common axis. A high order parameter means strongly aligned growth; near zero means randomly oriented spread.

Inputmask / grayscale
FromGeomorph
SourceBen-Jacob 1997

Angular power spectrum of boundary

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Build the distribution of branch/margin orientations around the colony and take its angular Fourier transform. Peaks at particular harmonics reveal an n-fold symmetry or a single preferred angle in how the margin branches.

Why this family survives dense, overlapping, low-resolution hyphae

Texture and anisotropy descriptors read branching density and directionality straight off the low-magnification grayscale image — they never try to find, separate, or count an individual branch. Because they aggregate pixel statistics and spatial-frequency energy over whole windows or the whole frame, signal accumulates rather than fragmenting, so they tolerate confluent, crossing, faint hyphae that defeat any skeleton.

That makes Family D the dependable fallback exactly when the mask itself is unreliable: GLCM/Haralick texture, FFT/Gabor/structure-tensor orientation, and the angular boundary spectrum all return useful numbers with no segmentation step in the loop — the 🟢 backbone for poorly resolved mycelium.
