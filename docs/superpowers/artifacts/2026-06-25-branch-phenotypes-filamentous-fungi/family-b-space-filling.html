The core ideas, drawn

Five mechanisms that recur across every metric below: how a slope becomes a dimension, why mass and surface diverge, what lacunarity adds, how the Euler number counts holes, and the morphotype axis these fits place a colony on.

Mass fractal dimension DBM (box-counting)

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Box-count the filled mask: the slope of log N(ε) vs log(1/ε) is DBM, a single space-filling index running ≈1.2 (sparse, foraging) → ≈2.0 (area-filling). The headline branchiness proxy.

Mass-vs-surface FD divergence

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Lacunarity Λ(r) (gliding-box)

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Dense-branching-morphology signature

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Two statistics flag the random tip-splitting (DBM) morphotype: branch width ≈ inter-branch gap (a single dominant length scale) plus an exponential branch-length distribution. Together they fingerprint dense space-filling by repeated tip splitting.

Why this family is the robust backbone for confluent mycelium

Every metric here is computed from the whole-mycelium silhouette and never separates one hypha from the next. Each one fits a slope, a variance, or an additive number by integrating over many spatial scales — so a handful of mis-thresholded pixels, a few crossing strands, or a soft low-resolution edge barely move the result.

That is exactly why three analog fields — neuroscience, plant-root architecture, and vascular imaging — all fall back to fractal dimension, lacunarity, density, and Minkowski descriptors the moment branches stop being separable. These are the 🟢 ship-now rows: they extend PhenoTypic's existing shape/texture toolkit, need only the mask or grayscale you already have, and require no branch retracing to be trustworthy on dense, overlapping, low-res growth.
