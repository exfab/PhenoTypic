Family · Regime A · skeleton + graph

Network topology

Once a colony can be reduced to a skeleton and a graph — nodes for tips and junctions, edges for segments — this family reads the wiring: how many tips, how often it branches, what order each segment sits at, and how many loops close. It is the architectural layer the rest of the catalog points toward, and the one most exposed to overlap: every count rides on a correctly resolved graph.

Reading a hyphal graph

Five primitives the family is built on: node degrees, Strahler ordering, the hyphal growth unit, anastomosis loops and the first Betti number, and Horton's bifurcation ratio. Each is hand-drawn below; the metric cards then put one phenotype per card.

Node degree: tip, junction, crossing

Regime A

Every skeleton node carries a degree = how many segments meet there. Degree-1 = a free tip; degree-3 = a true junction (a branch); degree-4 = a crossing — most often two hyphae passing over each other, not a branch.

Horton–Strahler ordering

Regime A

Terminal segments are order 1. Where two segments of the same order k meet, the downstream segment becomes order k+1; where unequal orders meet, the larger order continues. Order grows toward the trunk.

Hyphal Growth Unit (HGU)

Regime A

The classic mycology shape index: total length ÷ tip count — the mean length of hypha that supports one growing tip. Low HGU = a densely branched mat; high HGU = long sparse runners.

Anastomosis loop & first Betti number

Regime A

When two hyphae fuse they close a loop. The number of independent loops is the first Betti number β₁ = E − V + C (edges minus vertices plus connected components) — the count of genuinely cyclic, load-bearing connections.

Bifurcation ratio Rb

Regime A

Horton's law: count the segments at each Strahler order and take the ratio of successive counts, Rb = Nω / Nω+1. A near-constant ratio across orders is the signature of a self-similar branching tree.

These are the metrics that fail first under overlap

Family F is the most exposed family in the catalog. Because every count rides on a correctly resolved skeleton graph, overlap manufactures false junctions (two crossing hyphae read as a branch), hides tips (two apices fused into one node), and breaks the ordering (a single anastomosis loop destroys strict Strahler/centrifugal order). That is why all but the mask-level Euler number sit at 🟡 or 🔴 — they are retracing-gated, depending on the in-progress sdk_/branch_pathfinding least-cost-path engine that reconnects fragments and traverses crossings rather than mis-reading them.

The literature points at four concrete accuracy levers for this family:

  • Degree-4 resolution by direction continuity — continue each strand at the smallest turning angle, separating a true branch from a crossing from an anastomosis (the documented accuracy ceiling).
  • A vesselness cost term (Frangi/Sato) in the composite cost surface, recovering faint thin hyphae before thresholding so the graph starts cleaner.
  • Time-lapse disambiguation — a true fusion persists as a load-bearing loop while a chance crossing does not, turning ambiguous static junctions into resolvable events.
  • A DIADEM-style topology-similarity gate — validate traces against hand-traced ground truth before any of these counts ship.

Until those land, prefer the family's more forgiving members — total hyphal length, junction density, HGU, and the mask-level Euler number — and treat per-tip, per-order, and loop counts as aspirational on dense, low-resolution mycelium.
