Growth dynamics

The rate of everything else. Register a time-lapse and the static size, shape and topology descriptors turn into velocities: how fast the colony edge advances, how fast hyphal tips extend and split, and how the whole network densifies over time. Measurable only once frames are aligned — the spatial fit is inherited from the underlying quantity, then gated again by the need for registered time.

Colony-scale rates — from a plate time-series

Read off the advancing silhouette frame to frame. Every metric here is ⏱ but mask-only: no hyphae are separated, so they survive dense, overlapping, low-resolution growth.

Colony radial growth rate (Kr / Km)

⏱ DynMask🟢 B-robust

How fast the colony margin advances outward, dR/dt. After an initial lag the radius grows linearly with time, and that constant slope — the radial growth rate Kr (or Km on a per-colony basis) — is the single most reproducible growth descriptor a plate can give you.

Inputtime-lapse mask
Fitvery high (edge only) · 🟢⏱
FromMycology
SourceTrinci 1971

Radial expansion / colonization rate

⏱ DynMask🟢 B-robust

The rate at which colonized area grows, dArea/dt. Where Kr tracks the one-dimensional edge, this tracks the two-dimensional footprint — how quickly the colony claims new territory on the plate.

Specific (biomass) growth rate µ

⏱ DynMask🟡 A-feasible

The exponential rate of biomass accumulation, d ln(L) / dt, where L is total hyphal length (or a mask-derived biomass proxy). Unlike Kr it captures interior thickening, not just edge advance — the colony getting denser, not just bigger.

Peripheral growth-zone width w

⏱ DynMask🟢 B-robust

The width of the annular ring at the colony margin that actually drives radial extension, derived as w = Kr / µ. It is the band of metabolically active leading hyphae behind the front — wider w, faster spread per unit µ.

Mass-FD trajectory FD(t)

⏱ DynMask🟢 B-robust

The box-counting fractal dimension of the mask, tracked frame by frame. A rising FD(t) means the network is densifying — filling in the gaps between hyphae — while a flat FD means it is expanding self-similarly without getting denser.

Growth-front anisotropy

⏱ DynMask🟢 B-robust

How non-circular the advancing outline becomes over time — the ellipticity or eccentricity of the colony silhouette tracked across frames. A circle staying circular is isotropic spread; an outline stretching along one axis signals directional bias.

Space-filling / coverage rate

⏱ DynMask🟡 A-feasible

How fast the colony fills its reachable area, d(coverage)/dt, where coverage is the occupied fraction inside the convex hull or field of view. It blends expansion and densification into one saturating curve toward full occupancy.

Per-tip & per-branch rates — from tracked skeletons

These need registered time and a clean per-tip skeleton you can follow across frames — microscopy territory. They are the richest dynamics, and the most fragile on a dense plate.

Tip extension (elongation) rate

⏱ DynSkeleton🔴 A-fragile

How fast an individual hyphal tip advances along its own axis, d(tip position)/dt. Where Kr is the colony's collective edge speed, this is the elongation velocity of one apex — the cellular engine underneath the radial rate.

What a plate time-series gives you — and what needs the microscope

Every metric in this family carries the ⏱ clock, but they split sharply on the spatial fit. The dividing line is whether you ever separate individual hyphae.

Cheap, from a macroscopic plate time-series (mask-only ⏱ — ship-now): these read off the advancing silhouette and never resolve a strand, so they survive dense, overlapping, low-resolution growth exactly as the static Regime-B descriptors do.

  • Colony radial growth rate Kr/Km — slope of edge radius vs time.
  • Radial expansion / colonization rate — dArea/dt of the footprint.
  • Mass-FD trajectory FD(t) — box-counting dimension tracked per frame.
  • Growth-front anisotropy — ellipticity of the outline over time.
  • (adjacent, mask-feasible) specific growth rate µ, derived peripheral-zone width w, and coverage rate — robust but softening as the interior saturates.

Needs microscopy + per-tip tracking (🔴⏱ — aspirational on a plate): these require a clean, time-registered skeleton where each apex and junction is followed frame to frame — precisely the case dense overlap breaks.

  • Per-tip extension rate — displacement of one apex along its axis.
  • Branching rate & specific branching frequency — new branch points (per length) per time.
  • Apical vs lateral branching kinetics and first-branch lag — where and when branches form.
  • Anastomosis rate — persistent loop-forming fusions, disambiguated by time.
  • Negative autotropism and chemotropism / gradient bias — per-tip trajectory steering.

Practical rule: on a standard agar plate, reach for the mask-only rates; the per-tip dynamics belong to confocal / microfluidic acquisition or a future mature retracing module.
