A · Size & shape

Whole-silhouette morphometrics computed from the binary mask (no hyphal isolation). Net-new descriptors beyond the catalogued core (area, circularity, solidity, hull, eccentricity, Zernike, holes).

Boundary fractal dimension

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Box-counting on the silhouette border pixels: the slope of log N(ε) versus log(1/ε) over a geometric series of box sizes, quantifying how crenellated the outline is.

Why it's of interest

Separates two masks with identical area and perimeter but different margin waviness: a smooth ellipse and a lobed outline can share a perimeter yet D climbs only for the crenellated border. It complements mass FD by describing the edge rather than the interior.

Output metric
Value
Shape_FD_Boundary — box-counting dimension of the border
Units
dimensionless, ~1 (smooth) → ~2 (space-filling margin)
Reads as
higher ⇒ a more convoluted, rugged outline; ~1 ⇒ a near-smooth boundary
Method options
  1. box-count the border returned by skimage.measure.find_contours over a geometric ε series, regress log–log recommended
  2. Minkowski–Bouligand dilation dimension from successive boundary dilations
Variables
ε
box side length
N(ε)
boxes occupied by the border at scale ε
D
regression slope = fractal dimension
Inputbinary mask
SourceCryphonectria fractal silhouettes (10.1002/jobm.200800017)

Mass fractal dimension

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Box-counting or mass–radius scaling on the FILLED silhouette, measuring how completely the interior fills the plane rather than how ragged the edge is.

Why it's of interest

Resolves interior space-filling that boundary FD misses: two masks with an equally ragged margin but different interior compactness (a solid disc vs a filigreed web) diverge here. Reporting mass and box-count FD together flags scaling bias when they disagree.

Output metric
Value
Shape_FD_Mass — interior mass-scaling dimension
Units
dimensionless, ~1.7–2.0
Reads as
higher ⇒ the interior fills the plane more completely; lower ⇒ a sparse, web-like fill
Method options
  1. mass–radius: count pixels within radius r of the centroid and regress log M vs log r recommended
  2. box-count on the filled mask — report BOTH as a bias check
Variables
M
enclosed filled-pixel mass
r
radius from centroid
D
regression slope = fractal dimension
Inputbinary mask
SourceAspergillus niger mycelial fractal (10.1186/1475-2859-5-5)

Feret diameters & roundness

Regime B🟢 B-robust

The maximum and minimum caliper spans of the convex hull, plus Feret roundness 4A/(π·F_max²) — a rotation-invariant aspect ratio and disc-likeness measure.

Why it's of interest

Hull-based, so it stays stable where a ragged margin would bias moment axes: F_max/F_min gives a clean rotation-invariant aspect ratio, and Feret roundness distinguishes an elongated hull from a compact disc-like one independent of orientation.

Output metric
Value
Shape_MaxFeret, MinFeret, FeretRoundness — spans + disc-likeness
Units
MaxFeret/MinFeret px (µm with scale); FeretRoundness dimensionless 0–1
Reads as
F_max/F_min high ⇒ elongated hull; roundness → 1 ⇒ hull near a disc
Method options
  1. skimage.measure.regionprops.feret_diameter_max plus cv2.minAreaRect width recommended
  2. full rotating-calipers sweep over hull vertices for both extremes
Variables
F_max
maximum caliper span
F_min
minimum caliper span
A
mask area
Inputbinary mask
Sourcegrain morphometry (10.3390/agronomy8120296)

Elliptic Fourier descriptors (EFD)

Regime B🟡 feasible

A Fourier expansion of the closed contour into per-harmonic 4-coefficient ellipses, normalized to be invariant to rotation, scale and start point — a compact outline signature.

Why it's of interest

Where single scalars collapse shape to one number, EFD retains a spectrum: two masks with equal area, roundness and FD but different arrangements of lobes separate cleanly because low harmonics capture gross form and high harmonics capture fine margin structure.

Output metric
Value
Shape_EFD_{a,b,c,d}n for n harmonics + a reconstruction-error scalar
Units
dimensionless (normalized coefficients)
Reads as
energy concentrated in low n ⇒ smooth gross shape; power at high n ⇒ fine margin detail
Method options
  1. pyefd.elliptic_fourier_descriptors (Kuhl–Giardina), n≤10, normalized recommended
  2. reconstruct-and-compare IoU against the mask to choose the harmonic count
Variables
n
harmonic index
a,b,c,d
the four coefficients per harmonic
Inputbinary mask
SourceKuhl & Giardina (10.1016/0146-664X(82)90034-X)

LOCO-EFA lobe descriptors

Why it's of interest

Turns the opaque EFD spectrum into a countable protrusion tally, resolving the distinction between two masks with equal EFD energy but a different number of lobes — something neither convexity nor FD names directly.

higher lobe count ⇒ more distinct margin protrusions; larger amplitude ⇒ deeper lobing
Method options

  2. threshold the Ln spectrum to count significant lobes

Extent (rectangularity)

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Object area divided by its bounding-box area; an oriented variant divides instead by the minimum-area rotated rectangle for rotation invariance.

Why it's of interest

Cheaply resolves how rectangular versus sparse a shape is: a filled rectangle and a plus-sign can share the same bounding box yet differ sharply in extent. The oriented variant removes the axis-aligned bias that inflates extent for a diagonally elongated mask.

Output metric

Units
dimensionless 0–1
Reads as
low ⇒ the shape leaves much of its box empty; → 1 ⇒ box-filling / rectangular
Method options

  2. cv2.minAreaRect area for a rotation-invariant oriented extent

Convexity & concavity-depth spectrum

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Hull perimeter divided by object perimeter (→1 for convex shapes), plus the depth and count of the hull indentations where the contour dips inside its convex hull.

Why it's of interest

The only descriptor here directly sensitive to concave indentations: it separates a smooth convex blob from one bearing deep notches even when area, extent and FD match. Count plus max-depth distinguishes many shallow scallops from a single deep cleft.

Reads as
deeper / more defects ⇒ a more indented margin; convexity → 1 ⇒ near-convex outline
Method options

  2. cv2.convexityDefects for per-defect depth (prefer count + max at low resolution)

Hu moment invariants

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Seven algebraic combinations of normalized central moments, constructed to be invariant to translation, rotation and scale — a compact global shape signature.

Why it's of interest

Gives a pose-free fingerprint for matching or clustering masks that boundary metrics describe only piecewise: shapes that look identical after any rotation or rescale collapse to the same φ vector, so it complements orientation-dependent descriptors by canceling pose entirely.

Output metric
Value
Shape_Hu1..7 — log-transformed signed invariants
Units
dimensionless (log|φ|, signed)
Reads as
φ1–φ4 stable at low resolution; φ5–φ7 encode fine asymmetry but are noise-sensitive
Method options
  1. cv2.HuMoments on cv2.moments of the mask, use log|φ| recommended
  2. direct central-moment computation for a normalization cross-check
Variables
φ_i
the i-th Hu invariant (i = 1…7)
Inputbinary mask
SourceHu (10.1109/TIT.1962.1057692)

Contour bending energy

Regime B🟡 feasible

The mean squared boundary curvature around the contour — a size-independent measure of how finely wavy or kinked the margin is.

Why it's of interest

Isolates margin waviness from scale: two masks of very different size but the same fraction of wiggle per unit boundary score alike, whereas raw perimeter would not. It complements FD by weighting sharp local kinks quadratically rather than counting box occupancy.

Output metric
Value
Shape_BendEnergy = (1/N)Σκ² — normalized bending energy
Units
dimensionless (normalized)
Reads as
higher ⇒ a more finely wavy or kinked boundary; near 0 ⇒ a smooth, gently curved margin
Method options
  1. discrete curvature κ on a smoothed contour (spline or finite difference), take mean of κ² recommended
  2. multiscale estimate via curvature scale space
Variables
κ
boundary curvature at a contour point
N
number of contour points
Inputbinary mask
Sourcecurvature scale space (10.1109/34.149591)

PCA elongation & orientation

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Eigen-decomposition of the pixel-coordinate covariance matrix, yielding an axis ratio (elongation) and the principal angle of the shape.

Why it's of interest

Resolves directional stretch and pose that isotropic descriptors ignore: two masks with equal area and roundness but different aspect ratios separate on elongation, and orientation captures alignment that Hu moments deliberately cancel. Being area-weighted, it is more stable than hull calipers on ragged margins.

Output metric
Value
Shape_PCAElong = √(λ₂/λ₁), Orientation = principal angle
Units
PCAElong dimensionless 0–1; Orientation radians
Reads as
low elongation ⇒ isotropic; → 1 ⇒ strongly elongated along θ
Method options
  1. np.cov of mask coordinates → eigenvalues / eigenvectors recommended
  2. skimage.measure.regionprops inertia-tensor moments
Variables
λ₁ ≥ λ₂
covariance eigenvalues (major, minor)
θ
principal-axis angle
Inputbinary mask
Sourceshape/orientation (10.1371/journal.pone.0259413)

B · Space-filling & heterogeneity

Global descriptors of how the mask fills space and how heterogeneous that filling is — fractal, topological, and morphological measures, all mask/grayscale, no strand isolation.

Succolarity (directional)

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Flood-fills the binary mask from one edge and box-counts the invaded occupancy weighted by its distance (pressure) from that edge, quantifying how freely a fluid could percolate through the occupied phase in a chosen direction. Reported per axis (↓↑←→).

Why it's of interest

Supplies the missing THIRD fractal descriptor: it separates two fills with identical fractal dimension (mass) and lacunarity (gaps) but different through-connectivity, and is the only descriptor here encoding DIRECTIONAL penetrability of the occupied phase.

Output metric
Value
σ — succolarity per direction (↓↑←→), plus A=max−min
Units
σ ∈ [0,1] dimensionless; A dimensionless
Reads as
higher σ ⇒ more percolable along that axis; A>0 ⇒ direction-dependent occupied structure
Method options
  1. directional flood-fill + pressure-weighted box count skimage.segmentation.flood + box-count recommended
  2. per-axis connectivity via scipy.ndimage.label seeded from each edge
Variables
σ
succolarity (per direction)
A
anisotropy = max σ − min σ
Inputbinary mask
Sourcede Melo & Conci 2013 (10.1007/s11235-011-9657-3)

Granulometry / pattern spectrum

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Applies successive morphological openings with structuring elements of growing radius; the negative derivative of surviving area versus radius is a size distribution (pattern spectrum) of the mask's features.

Why it's of interest

Resolves the difference between fills of equal area fraction but different granularity: it exposes whether occupied mass sits in one dominant feature size or a broad mixture, a distinction single scalars like FD cannot encode.

Output metric

PS(r) — probability mass per size, plus mean size, spread, PS-entropy
Units
r in px; PS(r) dimensionless (sums to 1); entropy in bits
Reads as
sharp peak ⇒ one characteristic feature size; broad/multi-peak ⇒ mixed size structure
Method options
  1. opening sweep, PS(r) = −dArea/dr skimage.morphology.opening recommended
  2. distance-transform max histogram (void analogue via porespy)
Variables
r
opening (structuring-element) radius
PS
pattern spectrum
Inputbinary mask
Sourcegranulometry (10.1117/1.3099707)

Persistent-homology Betti curves

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Builds a cubical complex on the mask (or a sublevel filtration of its distance transform) and tracks the number of connected components β₀ and enclosed loops β₁ across the filtration, discounting boundary noise by feature persistence.

Why it's of interest

Directly COUNTS enclosed loops/voids that lacunarity captures only statistically, and separates a fill riddled with holes from a solid one of identical mass — persistence filtering keeps only topologically stable structure.

Output metric
Value
β₀(t), β₁(t) curves + total persistence, persistence entropy
Units
β counts (dimensionless); persistence in filtration units; entropy dimensionless
Reads as
high sustained β₁ ⇒ many stable enclosed loops; β₀ decay rate ⇒ how components merge
Method options
  1. cubical / distance-transform filtration gudhi.CubicalComplex recommended
  2. reduce diagrams to total persistence + persistence entropy (ripser)
Variables
β₀
connected components
β₁
enclosed loops

Inputbinary mask
SourcePH microstructure 2023 (10.1038/s41598-023-28985-3)

Rényi dimensions D1, D2 (Δ heterogeneity)

Regime B🟡 B-robust

Computes the generalized (Rényi) box-dimension spectrum, breaking out the information dimension D1 and correlation dimension D2 as first-class outputs alongside the capacity dimension D0.

Why it's of interest

Distinguishes a monofractal fill (uniform density scaling, D0≈D1≈D2) from a multifractal one where occupied mass concentrates unevenly across scales — a heterogeneity axis that a single box-dimension collapses away.

Output metric
Value
D1, D2 Rényi dimensions + Δ = D0 − D2
Units
all dimensionless
Reads as
Δ>0 ⇒ multiscale density heterogeneity; Δ≈0 (D0≈D1≈D2) ⇒ monofractal uniformity
Method options
  1. box partition, Rényi entropy vs box size; D1 from Shannon-entropy scaling, D2 from correlation integral numpy box-count regression recommended
  2. gate on ≥1 decade of box sizes + minimum fit R²
Variables
D_q
order-q Rényi dimension
Δ
D0 − D2 heterogeneity gap
Inputbinary mask
Sourcegeneralized dims (10.48550/arxiv.1310.2719)

Configurational (Boltzmann) entropy

Estimates the thermodynamic configurational entropy of the occupancy pattern — the number of microstates consistent with the arrangement — making it sensitive to clumping versus dispersion at a FIXED area fraction.

Why it's of interest

The clean "same composition, different arrangement" discriminator: it isolates spatial ORDER at constant area fraction, resolving clumped versus dispersed fills that mass-, gap-, and dimension-based metrics leave indistinguishable.

Local fractal-dimension map

Regime B🔴 B-robust

Slides a window across the mask running box-counting inside each to build a per-pixel FD field; only the field's DISPERSION (mean/std/entropy) is reported, never per-pixel dimension as ground truth.

Why it's of interest

Flags spatially varying texture — a fill whose roughness changes across the field versus one that is statistically homogeneous — a distinction global FD averages away. FRAGILE: small windows span <1 decade, so treat only field variability as signal and cross-check that the field mean matches the global FD.

Output metric

Units
dimensionless
Reads as
high dispersion ⇒ spatially varying texture; low ⇒ homogeneous roughness
Method options
  1. windowed box-count map → std/entropy numpy sliding box-count recommended, dispersion only
  2. differential box-counting per window
Variables

Wavelet-leader multifractal

Regime B🟡 B-robust

Computes 2D wavelet leaders on the grayscale field and regresses their log-cumulants across octaves to recover the dominant Hölder exponent and the multifractality strength, with bootstrap confidence intervals.

Why it's of interest

Separates a monofractal grayscale texture (single Hölder exponent, c2≈0) from a multifractal one with a spectrum of local regularities — more stable than histogram box-multifractal because leaders suppress grid-alignment artifacts.

Output metric

Units
dimensionless
Reads as
c1 ≈ dominant regularity; c2≤0 = multifractality strength (c2≈0 ⇒ monofractal); c3 = spectrum asymmetry
Method options

  2. bootstrap confidence intervals on the cumulants

C · Radial / Sholl profiles

How mask density varies with radius (and angle) from the inoculum/centroid. The key move for our operating point: area-based ring-mass metrics that survive strand touching, where intersection-count Sholl fails. ⟳ marks the angular/directional additions.

Ring-mass profile M(r)

Regime B🟢 B-robust

An area-based reformulation of the Sholl analysis: instead of counting skeleton crossings, sum the foreground area falling inside each concentric annulus around the object origin.

Why it's of interest

Because it integrates area rather than counting crossings, M(r) is provably invariant to the strand overlap and touching that corrupt intersection counts — two strands that merge still contribute their full area, so the profile does not under-count where structure is dense.

Output metric
Value
M(r) — per-annulus foreground mass; the anchor object from which AUC, moments and decay all derive
Units
px²/annulus (µm²/annulus with scale)
Reads as
the radial distribution of occupied area; where along the radius the structure carries its bulk
Method options
  1. polar unwrap then sum over angle per radius bin skimage.transform.warp_polar recommended
  2. bincount of a distance-transform radius map masked by foreground
Variables
r
radius from object origin
M(r)
foreground mass in the annulus at radius r
Inputbinary mask
SourceSholl decomposition (10.1016/j.celrep.2019.04.097)

2D Sholl heatmap S(r,θ) ⟳

Regime B🟢 B-robust

The joint radius×angle occupancy map produced by a single polar warp of the mask — the parent object from which every angular metric in this family is derived.

Why it's of interest

It resolves whether occupied area is spread uniformly around the origin or concentrated into angular sectors — a distinction that a purely radial profile collapses away. Because each cell measures area fraction, it inherits the same overlap-robustness as the ring-mass profile.

Method options

  2. intersection-count form of the heatmap (less robust to overlap)
Variables

radius from origin

Inputbinary mask

Angular occupancy anisotropy (ADC) ⟳

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Collapse S(r,θ) over radius to an angular histogram p(θ), then measure its concentration by the circular resultant-vector length — how one-sided the occupancy is.

Why it's of interest

It reduces the full angular field to a single scalar that separates isotropic spread from one-sided occupancy. The magnitude is rotation-invariant while the argument rotates with the mask, cleanly separating "how directional" from "which direction".

Output metric

ADC — resultant length ∈ [0,1] plus a preferred direction; 0 = isotropic, →1 = one-sided
Units
dimensionless (direction in degrees)
Reads as
how concentrated the structure is into one angular region versus evenly wrapped around the origin
Method options
  1. angular histogram then resultant length |Σ p(θ)e^{iθ}| numpy.angle recommended
  2. von-Mises fit for a unimodal concentration parameter

Why it's of interest

Two masks can share an identical M(r) yet differ in within-ring clumping; Λ(r) separates them by resolving evenly filled rings from ones broken into gappy clusters at the same radius. Because it works on area statistics it is unaffected by strand touching.

Radial profile moments

Why it's of interest

The moments compress the whole radial profile into a few shape descriptors: the skew sign alone separates a core-heavy distribution (positive) from an annular or margin-heavy one (negative). Being area-weighted, they carry the overlap-robustness of M(r) into scalar form.

Cumulative mass C(r) + quantile radii

Regime B🟢 B-robust

The cumulative distribution of M(r) — the running fraction of total mass enclosed within radius r — together with the radii that enclose 25%, 50% and 75% of the mass.

Why it's of interest

The CDF is monotone, so it denoises the ring-to-ring fluctuation of M(r) and yields r50 as a robust characteristic radius. The quantile spread (r75 − r25) resolves a tightly concentrated distribution from a radially spread one without depending on any single noisy annulus.

Output metric

Units
C(r) fraction ∈ [0,1]; radii in px (µm with scale)
Reads as
how radially concentrated the mass is; r50 is the median-mass characteristic radius
Method options
  1. cumulative sum normalized by total, interpolate quantile crossings numpy.cumsum recommended
  2. empirical-CDF interpolation via a monotone spline for sub-bin radii
Variables
C(r)

Strand-thickness proxy t(r)

Regime B🟡 B-robust

Ring mass divided by ring arc-count — a mean strand-width estimate at each radius obtained without ever computing a skeleton.

Why it's of interest

It recovers a thickness estimate purely from the mask. The numerator (mass) is robust, but the denominator (arc count) miscounts where strands touch or merge, collapsing several strands into one component and biasing thickness high — so the metric carries a flag wherever ring density is high enough for touching to be likely.

The build-once rule

Architect the measurers around these seven intermediates, not around 40 independent operations. Most of the catalog is a set of reductions over the polar heatmap, the orientation field, and the box-count cube — computing those three well is 80% of the work, and it guarantees the derived metrics stay mutually consistent (e.g. global FD must equal the area-weighted mean of the local-FD map).

Three gates on top of the phantoms

  • Scale-range gate — for any box-counting estimator, report the fit R² and the fitted decade span; refuse to emit a fractal dimension when clean scaling spans < 1 decade.
  • Cross-estimator agreement — mass-radius FD vs box-count FD must agree; monofractal inputs must give D0≈D1≈D2 and wavelet c2≈0. Disagreement flags estimator bias.
  • Cross-method agreement — where a mask is thin enough to skeletonize, overlay classic skeleton Sholl N(r) on the area-based M(r); agreement in the thin regime and controlled divergence as density rises calibrates exactly where intersection metrics break.

What else to consider

Design notes carried out of the research

  • Regime-B substitution principle. Wherever overlap is possible, prefer area/mass over intersection/skeleton counts — ring-mass over Sholl-intersection, field-coherence over per-strand curvature. This is the through-line that lets most of the catalog ship now, before retracing accuracy is solved.
  • Emit a confidence gate with every value, not just the number — fractal-fit R² over ≥1 decade, coherence as a turning-confidence weight, a node-exclusion flag for SOAM. A silent fractal dimension fit over <1 decade is the classic footgun.
  • Use grayscale (detect_mat), not only the binary mask, for the multifractal, structure-tensor, and wavelet-leader metrics — the intensity texture is already retained and carries information the threshold discards.
  • Three different "angular" things live here and must not be conflated: along-strand turning / curvature (Turning group), colony-scale angular anisotropy (ADC, Family C), and the bifurcation angle at a node (catalogued separately). Same graph in two cases, different inputs.
  • Two estimators beat one. Report SOAM and the distance metric together (one catches fine wiggle, the other gross arcing); report mass FD by both box-count and mass-radius. Redundancy is the cheapest bias check you have.
  • Citations flagged unverified in-page (Plotnick gliding-box DOI, SNT Angular-Sholl primary paper, Mokhtarian curvature-scale-space, the access-blocked Cell Reports full text) should be confirmed before external use — see the footer caveat.
