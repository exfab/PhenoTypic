Size & shape of the colony silhouette

The bedrock descriptors: how big the colony is and what gross outline it takes. Every one is read straight off the binary mask — no individual hyphae need to be resolved — which is why this family is the most robust, ship-now layer and already largely lives in PhenoTypic's SHAPE/SIZE schema.

Projected area

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Total colony footprint: the number of foreground pixels in the mask multiplied by the physical area of one pixel. The single most direct, very-high-robustness measure of how much surface the mycelium occupies.

Inputbinary mask
FromMycologyRoots
SourcePaul & Thomas 1998

Perimeter

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Length of the colony boundary, measured by walking the chain-coded contour pixel-to-pixel around the mask edge. Medium robustness only — it is scale-sensitive, growing as resolution exposes more of a ragged filamentous margin.

Inputbinary mask
FromMycologyRoots
SourcePaul & Thomas 1998

Equivalent diameter

Regime B🟢 B-robust

The diameter a perfect circle would need to enclose the same area as the colony: √(4A/π). A single, high-robustness size scalar that lets colonies of any outline be compared on one length axis.

Inputbinary mask
FromMycology
SourcePapagianni & Mattey 2006

Circularity / form factor

Regime B🟢 B-robust

How disc-like the silhouette is: 4πA/P², which equals 1 for a perfect circle and falls toward 0 as the margin sends out filaments and lobes (inflating P relative to A). A medium-robustness compact-vs-filamentous index.

Inputbinary mask
FromMycology
SourcePaul & Thomas 1998

Solidity / convexity / fullness

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Ratio of the colony's actual area to its convex-hull area. ≈1 for a compact disc; well below 1 when a filamentous margin leaves large concave voids inside the hull — a high-robustness compact-vs-diffuse discriminator.

Inputbinary mask
FromMycologyRootsNeuro
SourceTucker & Thomas 1992

Convex-hull area / territory

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Area of the smallest convex polygon that wraps the entire colony — the "territory" the mycelium has reached, including the empty gaps between outermost filaments. A high-robustness measure of total spatial reach.

Inputbinary mask
FromRootsNeuro
SourceGalkovskyi 2012

Hull occupancy / coverage

Regime B🟢 B-robust

How fully the colony fills the territory it reaches: network (mask) area divided by convex-hull area. High values mean a densely packed front; low values mean a sparse, space-filling exploration of the same reach. High robustness.

Inputbinary mask
FromNeuroRoots
SourceSholl/SNT

Eccentricity / aspect ratio

Regime B🟢 B-robust

Elongation of the silhouette, from the best-fit ellipse: the ratio of its foci distance (eccentricity, 0 = circle → 1 = line) or of its axis lengths. High robustness — it captures directional, oval growth that circularity alone misses.

Inputbinary mask
FromMycologyRoots
SourcePaul & Thomas 1998

Major / minor ellipse axes

Regime B🟢 B-robust

The two axis lengths of the same best-fit ellipse, reported as absolute sizes rather than a ratio: the long-axis extent and the short-axis extent of the colony. High robustness, and they give eccentricity its physical scale.

Inputbinary mask
FromRoots
SourceGalkovskyi 2012

Width / depth / width:depth

Regime B🟢 B-robust

The colony's bounding-box extents — its horizontal width and vertical depth — and their ratio. The ratio captures spread anisotropy relative to the image axes (e.g. growth biased along a gradient). High robustness.

Inputbinary mask
FromRootsNeuro
SourceGalkovskyi 2012

Centroid / radius of gyration

Regime B🟢 B-robust

The mass centre of the silhouette (centroid), and how far that mass spreads around it: the radius of gyration √(Σr²/N), the root-mean-square distance of occupied pixels from the centroid. High robustness; large when growth pushes mass outward.

Inputbinary mask
FromMycologyRootsGeomorph
SourceCox & Thomas (in Paul & Thomas 1998)

Zernike shape moments

Regime B🟢 B-robust

An orthogonal, rotation-invariant signature of the whole shape: the mask is projected onto a basis of Zernike polynomials on the unit disc, and the coefficient magnitudes form a compact fingerprint that compares shapes regardless of orientation. High robustness.

Inputbinary mask
FromMycology
SourceCellProfiler

Enclosed holes / mean hole size

Regime B🟢 B-robust

The count and mean area of background regions that are fully enclosed by the mycelium — interior gaps the colony has grown around. Medium robustness: small holes can vanish when resolution is low.

Inputbinary mask
FromRootsGeomorph
SourceSeethepalli 2021

Why this family survives dense, overlapping, low-resolution hyphae

Every metric here reads off the binary mask alone — none of them ever tries to separate one hypha from another. Because they never count tips, junctions, angles, or loops, they cannot be derailed by the crossings and confluence that break skeleton-graph metrics first. They are exactly the Regime-B descriptors three independent analog fields (neuroscience, root architecture, vascular) fall back to when structures cannot be resolved.

Robustness does vary within the family, and the catalog flags it: area, equivalent diameter, solidity, convex hull, eccentricity, axes, width/depth, and radius of gyration are very-high to high robustness because they depend on bulk pixel counts and outer extent. Two are weaker by construction — perimeter is scale-sensitive (a raggeder margin lengthens it, so fix the pixel scale), and enclosed holes are medium robustness because small interior gaps are lost at low resolution. These size and shape descriptors are the safe, ship-now backbone for confluent mycelium, and most of them already live in PhenoTypic's existing SHAPE/SIZE schema.
